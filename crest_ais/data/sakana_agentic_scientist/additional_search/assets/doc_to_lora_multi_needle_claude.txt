Addressing Beats Architecture:
Query-Conditioned Merge-Time Selection for
Context-to-LoRA Hypernetworks on
Multi-Needle Retrieval
The Agentic Scientist System



A context-to-LoRA hypernetwork reads a document once and emits a low-rank adapter that folds it into a
frozen language model. To serve inputs longer than its training chunk, the standard recipe splits the context,
generates one adapter per chunk, and merges the per-chunk adapters into a single higher-rank adapter
before the query is seen. We show that this merge, not the adapter generator or the training loss, is what
limits retrieval when a context holds several facts and only one is queried. On a synthetic multi-needle
needle-in-a-haystack benchmark spanning 128 to 65,536 tokens, we compare four mechanism families under
one fixed base model, data generator, and three-hour single-GPU budget. Redesigning the hypernetwork’s
read-out and output head plateaus at 0.32 exact-match accuracy, and auxiliary binding and selection losses
reach only 0.46; the strongest query-blind merge attains 0.52. Adding a query-conditioned late-interaction
(MaxSim) address that selects which chunk’s adapter dominates the merge – with no new parameters and
no retraining – lifts accuracy to 0.98. The bottleneck is selection at composition time, not head capacity or
auxiliary losses: weight-space context internalization is, at long context, a routing problem best resolved
when the adapter is composed.


Introduction
Folding in-context information directly into a model’s weights, instead of re-reading it as tokens at
every query, is an appealing route to low-latency adaptation, and a growing line of work internalizes
context into parameters or a compact learned memory rather than keeping it in the prompt (Cao
et al., 2025). A hypernetwork reads a context once and emits a low-rank adaptation (LoRA) that,
added to a frozen base model, makes it behave as though the context had been placed in the prompt.
This “context-to-LoRA” construction is the weight-space analogue of parameter-efficient fine-tuning
(Ding et al., 2022; Han et al., 2024; Mao et al., 2024), of soft-prompt transfer between frozen models
(Vu et al., 2022), and of hypernetwork-generated adapters for instruction following (Ivison et al.,
2023). Because the generator is trained at a bounded chunk length, the recipe scales to longer
inputs by partitioning the context into chunks, emitting one adapter per chunk, and merging the
per-chunk low-rank factors along the rank axis, so a longer context simply yields a higher-rank
adapter. Composing low-rank modules in this way connects to a broad literature on merging and
arithmetic combination of adapters (Huang et al., 2023; Kawata et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2024a; Zhang
et al., 2023).
Convenient as it is, this composition silently makes a retrieval decision. The merged adapter is
assembled before the query is known, so every chunk’s contribution enters inference on equal footing.
We probe this regime with a controlled multi-needle needle-in-a-haystack (NIAH) benchmark, the
synthetic long-context retrieval paradigm now standard for stress-testing how faithfully models
recover facts as context grows (Du et al., 2025; Hsieh et al., 2024; Roberts et al., 2024): each context
embeds one to four named facts of the form “the needle named amber stores magic number 1234,” with
names and numbers unique within a context, and the prompt requests a single target needle’s
four-digit number. A lone fact is retrieved almost perfectly, but as co-resident facts accumulate

a query-blind merge superposes their digits and the model returns a distractor’s number – a
binding/superposition failure, the interference that arises when distributed representations must
hold several bound key–value pairs at once (Elhage et al., 2022; Greff et al., 2020; Oberauer, 2009),
that added generation capacity, by itself, does not remove.
Our question is diagnostic rather than incremental: on a fixed substrate and budget, which
component governs multi-needle accuracy – the adapter-generating head, the training objective, the
magnitude prior, or the query-blind way adapters are merged? We evaluate four mechanism
families under an identical three-hour single-GPU budget on five context-length bins from 128 to
65,536 tokens. Two families target the intuitive suspects: the inductive bias of the hypernetwork’s
read-out and output head, and training-side objectives that supervise binding or selection. A third
is the strongest query-blind merge we could construct, combining redundancy weighting,
competitive gating, and magnitude-prior stabilization. The fourth makes composition query-aware:
a frozen-encoder late-interaction score, in the spirit of ColBERT-style MaxSim retrieval (Khattab
et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2024; Santhanam et al., 2022b), selects which chunk’s adapter is merged for
the current query over delimiter-aligned chunk boundaries.
The result is sharp and one-sided. Read-out and head redesign stays near 0.32 exact-match, auxiliary
supervision reaches 0.46, and the best query-blind merge reaches 0.52; none of the three query-blind
families crosses 0.52. Making selection query-conditioned at merge time – alone – lifts accuracy to
0.91, and adding relevance-profile atom placement and delimiter-aligned chunk cuts reaches 0.98 (§).
Our contributions are: (i) a decomposition that localizes the multi-needle bottleneck to query-blind
adapter composition rather than the hypernetwork head or the training loss; (ii) a parameter-free,
merge-time late-interaction selection mechanism, plus two chunk-level components, that raises
accuracy from a 0.52 ceiling to 0.98 without changing the base model, the adapter generator, or
the training objective; and (iii) two grounded negative-control families that make the attribution
falsifiable.

Related Work
Context internalization and hypernetwork adapters. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning adapts a
frozen model with a small set of added weights (Ding et al., 2022; Han et al., 2024; Mao et al.,
2024), and soft-prompt transfer shows that adaptation signal can be moved between frozen models
through a compact learned artifact (Vu et al., 2022). Hypernetworks go further by generating the
adaptation from an input rather than learning it by gradient descent; HINT, for instance, encodes an
instruction into adapter weights for zero- and few-shot generalization (Ivison et al., 2023). Context-
to-LoRA applies the same principle to arbitrary context, reading a document once and emitting
an adapter that internalizes it. We take this generator as fixed and ask how its per-chunk outputs
should be combined at long context length.

Merging and composing low-rank modules. A large body of work composes adapters or full
models after training: dynamic LoRA composition (Huang et al., 2023), arithmetic over parameter-
efficient modules (Klimaszewski et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2023), weight-ensembling mixtures and
representation-surgery merges (Tang et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024b), attribute-conditioned LoRA
merging (Kawata et al., 2026), and broad surveys of model merging (Yang et al., 2024a). These
methods typically fix the merge coefficients once, independent of the query at inference. Our setting
differs in that the correct composition depends on the query: the adapter that should dominate is
the one whose chunk contains the queried fact, which is unknown until inference. We therefore
make the composition itself query-conditioned.

Late interaction and token-level retrieval. Late-interaction retrieval scores a query against a
passage by summing token-level maximum similarities (MaxSim), retaining fine-grained matches
that single-vector encoders discard (Khattab et al., 2021; Santhanam et al., 2022b); follow-up work
analyzes its matching behavior and efficiency (Liu et al., 2024; Santhanam et al., 2022a), and dense-
retrieval encoders study token- versus passage-level granularity (Ma et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023;
Wu et al., 2023; Zhong et al., 2022). Retrieval-augmented generation uses such scores to fetch
external text and reduce hallucination (Shuster et al., 2021). We reuse the MaxSim primitive in an
unusual place: not to fetch text into a prompt, but to decide, inside a frozen encoder’s representation
space, which generated adapter to merge into the weights. To our knowledge, late interaction has
not previously been used as a merge-time router over hypernetwork-generated adapters.

Method
Problem setup
The system is a context-to-LoRA hypernetwork over a frozen instruction-tuned ∼2B-parameter
decoder language model. Given a context c, a latent cross-attention encoder produces per-chunk
summaries and a hypernetwork emits a low-rank adapter ∆Wk for each chunk k, applied to a single
projection family of the frozen model at a fixed rank. To serve contexts longer than the training
chunk length, the per-chunk adapters are merged into one higher-rank adapter that is added to the
base weights, after which the model is queried with no context tokens in the prompt. The task
is multi-needle NIAH: each context independently samples one to four named facts (“the needle
named name stores magic number dddd”), names and numbers are unique within a context, and
the prompt requests exactly one target needle’s four-digit number. Distractor needles are placed
at random non-target depths, so a system must learn key–value binding rather than positional
copying. The optimization target is exact-match accuracy on the queried number, evaluated on
five context-length bins spanning 128 to 65,536 tokens; every configuration is trained within a strict
three-hour single-GPU budget and scored by one fixed protocol.

Where the bottleneck could live
Multi-needle retrieval can fail at three places, and our hypotheses map directly to them. (1)
Generation: the hypernetwork’s read-out or output head might lack the capacity to encode several
distinct key–value bindings, in which case richer heads should help. (2) Training: the loss might
never teach the model to separate co-resident facts, in which case auxiliary binding or selection
objectives should help. (3) Composition: even with perfectly generated per-chunk adapters, merging
them query-blind superposes the facts, so the answer collapses toward an arbitrary co-resident
number. Our central hypothesis is (3): the merge is a retrieval decision made with no access to the
query, and no amount of generation capacity or training pressure can recover a decision that the
composition rule structurally discards. Making composition query-conditioned should therefore be
decisive, and families (1) and (2) should act as negative controls that stay below the query-blind
ceiling.

Compared approaches
We evaluate four mechanism families on the same substrate; configurations within each are grouped
by their dominant mechanism.

Query-blind adapter merge (baseline ceiling). The reference composition concatenates the per-
chunk low-rank factors along the rank axis and adds the result to the frozen weights. We include the
strongest query-blind instance we could build: redundancy-aware chunk weighting that suppresses
near-duplicate chunks, a competitive per-slot gate, and a gradient-pinned magnitude prior that

keeps the merged adapter’s write norm in a healthy range. This family fixes the accuracy ceiling
that a decisive mechanism must beat.

Query-conditioned late-interaction selection (proposed). At inference we encode the query and
each context chunk with the same frozen early-exit encoder that already feeds the hypernetwork,
and score each chunk against the query with a token-level late-interaction address: for every query
token we take its maximum cosine similarity to the chunk’s token features and average over query
tokens (Khattab et al., 2021; Santhanam et al., 2022b). This MaxSim address selects which chunk’s
generated adapter dominates the merge, turning query-blind composition into query-conditioned
selection. It adds no parameters and no training: it is a retrieval score computed in the frozen
encoder’s representation space and applied only when the adapter is composed, which is exactly
what isolates “selection” from “head capacity” and “training objective.”

Relevance-profile placement and delimiter-aligned chunking (proposed, supporting). Two
components convert a correct selection into a correct answer. First, a token-level relevance profile
places the merged adapter’s “atom” – the span whose representation the selected adapter is built
from – on a relevance peak rather than a content-blind window, so the queried fact, not a neighbor,
drives the adapter. Second, chunk boundaries are cut at the generator’s own delimiter units, so a
short target fact is not split across two chunks; a split fact reaches the adapter as a fragment and is
the dominant residual error once selection is query-conditioned.

Read-out and output-head redesign (negative control). This family changes the inductive bias of
adapter generation while leaving composition query-blind: scale-invariant slot read-outs,
decorrelated read keys, uniqueness-weighted token pooling, and factorized or jointly-normalized
key–value heads that alter how the hypernetwork emits its low-rank factors. If generation capacity
were the bottleneck, these should close the gap.

Auxiliary training supervision (negative control). This family adds selection pressure through
the training objective while leaving composition query-blind: multi-target needle supervision that
grades several needles from one context, a co-resident digit-margin loss that penalizes emitting a
distractor’s digits, supervised chunk addressing, and cross-context distractor blocks that inject hard
negatives. If the loss were the bottleneck, these should close the gap.
We additionally report a training-reliability component – magnitude-prior pinning and an early
mode-detection restart – that is not an accuracy driver: it reduces a run-to-run collapse mode in
which training occasionally converges to a degenerate adapter scoring zero. We treat it strictly as a
robustness aid and discuss it under limitations.

Experimental protocol
Each configuration is one trained model evaluated once by the fixed benchmark. Training must
finish within three hours on a single GPU; over-budget runs are invalid and discarded. The base
model, data generator, evaluation scripts, and train/eval scale are identical across configurations, so
the only variable is the model-internal mechanism. We report exact-match accuracy on the queried
needle, pooled over the five context-length bins. Because the study varies one mechanism at a
time on a shared substrate, we read the four families as a controlled comparison and the proposed
method’s internal components as a mechanism attribution – which component adds how much
accuracy – not as a temporal trajectory. Of 41 model variants, 31 configurations were trained and
scored and 30 produced a valid model (one failed the runtime/validity contract and is excluded). A
subset of otherwise valid seeded runs collapsed to zero exact-match under a run-to-run instability

Implementation notes
The frozen base is an instruction-tuned ∼2B-parameter decoder; adapters are applied at a fixed low
rank to a single projection family, and the encoder is a latent cross-attention pooler that summarizes
each chunk. The late-interaction address and the relevance profile reuse this same frozen encoder,
so the proposed selection adds no trainable parameters and only a small inference-time encoding
cost, bounded well inside the evaluation budget. The negative-control families and the baseline
share the identical generator, encoder, and training loop; they differ only in the read-out/head, the
auxiliary loss terms, or the merge rule.

Threats to validity
The study is on a single synthetic benchmark with a fixed base model and a fixed three-hour budget;
absolute numbers may not transfer to natural long-context retrieval. The proposed address operates
in a frozen encoder’s representation space, so its ceiling is bounded by that encoder’s ability to
separate chunks, and a small short-context residual remains. We mitigate confounding by holding
every non-mechanism factor fixed and by including two independent negative-control families
rather than a single contrast.

Results
Main result: composition is the bottleneck
Figure 2 and Table 1 compare the best exact-match accuracy attained by each mechanism family.
Redesigning the hypernetwork read-out and output head leaves accuracy near 0.32; adding auxiliary
training supervision reaches 0.46; the strongest query-blind merge reaches 0.52. Making adapter
selection query-conditioned at merge time reaches 0.98 – an absolute gain of 0.46 over the best
query-blind system and of 0.66 over the best read-out/head redesign. The two intuitive suspects,
generation capacity and training loss, both stay strictly below the query-blind merge itself, which is
the strongest evidence that the bottleneck is neither: what is missing cannot be supplied by a richer
head or a better loss while composition remains query-blind.

Component attribution
Figure 3 decomposes the proposed method into cumulative mechanisms above the query-blind
ceiling. Starting from the 0.52 query-blind merge, adding query-conditioned late-interaction
selection alone lifts accuracy to 0.91 – by far the single largest jump in the study, and direct
confirmation that the missing ingredient is query-aware routing rather than anything upstream.
Placing the merged adapter’s atom on a relevance peak adds a further step to 0.93, and cutting
chunk boundaries at generator delimiter units so a target fact stays intact reaches 0.98. The two
supporting components together contribute the final 0.07: once the right chunk is selected, the
remaining errors are a correctly selected chunk whose atom is mis-placed or whose fact was split by
a boundary, both of which the supporting components address. An intermediate delimiter-snapped
span variant reaches 0.94 before aligned block cuts, confirming the two chunk-level components act
on the same residual.

The spread also exposes the run-to-run collapse mode: several otherwise-valid configurations, in
every family, score zero exact-match. These collapses are a training-stability artifact – a degenerate
adapter that never escapes an uninformative solution – rather than evidence about a mechanism,
and they include configurations from the proposed family itself. This is why we treat the reliability
component as robustness rather than accuracy: an early mode-detection restart and magnitude-prior
pinning recover collapsed draws to bank level (0.93) and reduce the frequency of zero-accuracy
runs, but they do not raise any family’s accuracy ceiling.

Interpretation
Read together, the three query-blind families give a falsifiable attribution. If generation capacity
were limiting, the read-out and head redesigns would have crossed the ceiling; they did not (best
near 0.32). If the training objective were limiting, auxiliary binding and selection losses would have
crossed it; they did not (≤ 0.46). The strongest query-blind merge, with redundancy weighting,
competitive gating, and magnitude stabilization, itself stops at 0.52. Only the mechanism that injects
query information at composition time crosses the ceiling, and by a wide margin. This localizes
the multi-needle bottleneck to query-blind adapter composition and identifies late-interaction
merge-time selection as the mechanism that resolves it.

Limitations
Our evidence is confined to one synthetic multi-needle benchmark, a single frozen base model,
and a fixed three-hour budget; the exact numbers, and the size of the gap, may not transfer to
natural documents with paraphrased or entailed facts where exact-match binding is ill-defined.
The proposed address is a retrieval score in a frozen encoder’s representation space, so it inherits
that encoder’s chunk-separability and cannot recover a fact the encoder cannot distinguish; a small
short-context residual, dominated by facts split across a chunk boundary, remains even after atom
placement and aligned chunking. The method assumes a chunked, mergeable adapter and a single
queried fact per prompt; extending query-conditioned composition to all-needles recall, to multiple
simultaneous queries, or to a learned (rather than frozen) address is left open. Finally, training-run
collapse remains a reliability concern that our stabilization only mitigates, and we report it as
variance rather than as a solved problem.

Conclusion
We asked where the accuracy bottleneck lives for a context-to-LoRA hypernetwork that must retrieve
one queried fact from a multi-needle context, and answered it by controlled comparison rather
than by adding capacity. Reshaping the hypernetwork’s read-out and output head, and adding
training-side binding and selection objectives, both leave accuracy at or below a 0.52 query-blind
ceiling. The decisive change is to make adapter composition query-conditioned: a late-interaction
(MaxSim) address in the frozen encoder’s space selects which chunk’s generated adapter is merged,
and with relevance-profile atom placement and delimiter-aligned chunk boundaries this raises
exact-match accuracy from 0.52 to 0.98 across context lengths from 128 to 65,536 tokens, without
touching the base model, the adapter generator, or the training loss. The broader lesson is that

– selection over generation and supervision – informs the design of scalable context-to-weight
