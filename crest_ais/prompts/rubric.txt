NEW — what is original about the substantive contribution?

Assess each answer's central contribution, including its claims, mechanisms,
mappings, relationships, and combinations. Familiar components can form an
original relationship or substantive synthesis; assess what the combination
contributes rather than treating its components' familiarity as decisive. An
obscure peripheral detail does not make the central contribution more original.

Use relevant prior work you can actually identify from existing knowledge as
evidence. Name a specific work, paper, standard formulation, subfield, or genre
only when you can support that connection; do not invent a source or look one
up. Distinguish a close precedent for the contribution from a broad thematic
resemblance. Explain what appears familiar and what the answer adds. Failure
to recognize or recall a precedent is uncertainty, not proof of historical
novelty; unfamiliar wording alone does not establish originality.


USEFUL — how well does it serve this task?

Interpret usefulness relative to the question's purpose, stated requirements,
and requested stage of development. Assess how each answer's central proposal
or contribution works, whether its mechanism is explained or merely asserted,
and whether the evidence and reasoning support the claimed payoff. An
exploratory proposal need not meet a finished product's evidence standard;
claimed established results require appropriate support.

Accept counterfactual, fictional, or hypothetical premises explicitly stipulated
by the question. Evaluate coherence, effectiveness, and fit within those
premises. A coined concept, invented example, or proposed mechanism is not an
error simply because it is invented. Distinguish such proposals from claims
presented as established facts, such as citations, statistics, experimental
results, attributions, or historical events. A load-bearing false factual claim
or unsupported claim of demonstrated effectiveness weakens the contribution.
State important uncertainty without turning every untested proposal into a
factual error.


SURPRISING — how does its approach depart from the expected one?

Briefly identify the obvious approach a competent answer would most likely take
to this question. Compare each answer's substantive approach with that baseline,
considering whether its unexpected move is central or incidental. A meaningful
new combination, a deeper development of an expected direction, or a convincing
challenge to the obvious approach can depart from expectation while staying
within the task's premises.

Unexpected wording, arbitrary additions, and incoherence are not substantive
creative advantages. Mere unsupported or incoherent surprise cannot on its own
establish stronger overall creative quality. Weigh an unexpected idea together
with what it contributes and how well it works for the requested task; do not
treat surprise as independent of the rest of the overall judgment.
