4. Why the Sarrus entry is a scoring bug, not a citation slip

C5_engineering_48's query asks for "an entirely original planar linkage mechanism: a named kinematic chain of rigid links joined by revolute and/or prismatic joints…". known_answers #1 then states the gate: "New is judged against mechanisms whose DEFINING principle matches any entry below." Entry #10 is the Sarrus linkage — a spatial 6R mechanism.

Two concrete failure modes follow:

  1. False non-novelty. A submission proposing a genuinely new planar rotation-to-translation linkage can be scored down for "matching the defining principle" of Sarrus — a mechanism that cannot exist in the plane. The exclusion set is over-broad by one entry, and it is over-broad in exactly the direction (straight-line/translation output) that planar-linkage inventions most often target. Note that #6/#7/#8/#15/#16/#17 already cover planar straight-line generation properly (Peaucellier-Lipkin, Watt, Chebyshev, Roberts, Hart, Scott Russell), so #10 adds no legitimate coverage.
  2. Contradictory instruction to the answering model. A model reading the prior-art list as an implicit definition of scope can reasonably conclude that spatial mechanisms are admissible, then answer out of class and be penalized under hard_rules for a constraint the catalogue itself blurred. That is an item-design defect, distinguishable from a wrong date.

Secondary, softer issue in the same list: #15 "Roberts linkage" (approximate straight line) is Richard Roberts (1789-1864), while #21 "Roberts-Chebyshev cognate linkages" is Samuel Roberts (1827-1913). The list does not conflate them, but a judge or model easily might; disambiguating the first names would harden the entry.

5. Systemic pattern: brand date vs. concept date

Six of the eleven IMPRECISE findings are the same defect — a single year attached to a named artifact where the name and the underlying concept have different birthdays:

Entry Concept date Name/brand date Listed
Case-Shiller late 1980s method / 1991 company 2002 (S&P/Case-Shiller) 1991
Baltic Dry Index 1985 (BFI) 1999 (BDI) 1985
STAR voting 2014 (SRV) ~2018 (STAR) ~2014
Bucklin voting 1793 (Condorcet) 1909 (Bucklin/Grand Junction) ~1909
Kemeny-Young 1959 (Kemeny) 1978/1988 (Young) 1959
ICLAC Register 2010 (register) 2012 (ICLAC) 2012
Finland Cttee for the Future 1993 (temporary) 2000 (permanent standing) 1993
Dutch colonial-collections framework 2020 (report) 2021 policy / 2022 committee 2021

This is a systematic novelty-gate risk, not cosmetic. Every C5 known_answers list opens with "New is judged against <indices/methods/mechanisms> whose DEFINING principle matches any entry below". When the listed year is the branding year, the gate silently understates how long the defining principle has been public — and a submission whose concept was anticipated in 1793 (Bucklin) or 1985 (BFI) can be scored as novel against a 1909/1999 prior-art boundary. The fix is cheap and mechanical: for every catalogue entry, record the earliest publication of the defining principle, and put the brand name/date second.

A structural observation about the not-audited set. The five later-numbered C4 items (_42, _43, _44, _45) and C5_arts_humanities_49 have bare, undated catalogues — e.g. C4_materials_chemistry_43 lists "E-factor (Sheldon)", "Atom economy (Trost)", "EcoScale route-assessment score" with no years, no venues, no citations; C4_engineering_45 lists "iFixit Repairability Score", "EN 45554", "Modularity index (Martin & Ishii)" likewise. These are low-risk for date errors precisely because they assert almost nothing — but that is also why they are weak novelty gates: they cannot tell a judge which version, edition, or formulation of a method counts as prior art. Compare the _1x/_2x items, whose entries carry full citations and explicit ALLOWED/FORBIDDEN annotations (C4_biology_medicine_18 is the strongest in the set). The catalogue quality across C4/C5 is bimodal, and the bimodality tracks item ID, which suggests two different authoring passes. That inconsistency will produce inconsistent novelty scoring across domains regardless of whether any individual entry is right.

7. Overall assessment: are the C4/C5 prior-art catalogues trustworthy enough to gate novelty?

Qualified yes for the richly-cited items; not yet for the whole set. Ship after the §3 edits.

What holds up. Zero fabrications across 33 live claim-checks spanning eight domains — no invented papers, no invented institutions, no invented indices, no misattributed authorship (the one authorship issue, "Weyl-Posner", is a reversed order of two real co-authors, and the prior pass's "Lanier-Weyl → Posner & Weyl" correction was itself correct). Hard-to-get details are right at a rate I did not expect: NAGPRA's enactment date to the day (1990-11-16) and its 2024 regulatory revision; the WIPO GRATK Treaty adoption date (2024-05-24); HHI's correct 1945/1950 dual attribution; Digital Benin's institution count; Cellosaurus down to volume and pages; and a post-cutoff March 2026 Swiss restitution agreement dated exactly right. The expert_baseline fields are clean. That is a catalogue built by someone who checked their work.

What does not hold up.

  1. The brand-vs-concept date defect (§5) is systematic and directly corrupts the gate. It appears in at least six entries and, unlike a typo, it biases in a consistent direction: it makes prior art look younger than it is, which makes submissions look more novel than they are. For C5 items — whose entire scoring premise is "no established X already exists on this defining concept" — this is the single most consequential class of finding in this audit.
  2. C5_engineering_48 has a live scoring bug, not a citation error. A spatial mechanism sits in the exclusion set for a planar-only invention task, adding no legitimate coverage (planar straight-line generators are already covered six ways) while creating both a false-non-novelty path and a contradictory scope signal to the answering model. This one should block release of that item until fixed.
  3. Catalogue depth is bimodal by item ID (§6). C4_biology_medicine_18 carries per-entry ALLOWED / FORBIDDEN INPUT / PARTIALLY FORBIDDEN annotations that make the gate genuinely operational; C4_engineering_45 is a list of twelve bare names. Both are asked to perform the same function. They will not perform it equally, and cross-domain score comparisons will inherit that asymmetry.
  4. One post-cutoff entry is a moving target. C4_arts_humanities_23 #16 hard-codes March 2026 Benin restitution events that were already superseded by a June 2026 handover. This needs a snapshot date or a per-release re-verification policy.

Recommended gate before public release:

  • Blocking: apply the C5_engineering_48 #10 fix; apply the six brand-vs-concept date fixes in §3.
  • Blocking: live-verify the oncolytic-virus approval date in C5_biology_medicine_24 #19 and drop the unsupported "at Nigeria's request" in C4_arts_humanities_23 #16.
  • Strongly recommended: adopt a catalogue convention — earliest publication of the defining principle first, brand name and date second — and sweep all 20 items for it. This is the one change that converts the catalogues from "accurate bibliography" into "sound novelty gate".
  • Recommended: bring the _4x items up to _1x/_2x citation depth, or explicitly document that their novelty gate is coarser so scores are not compared across the two tiers.
  • Recommended: finish claim-level coverage on the 9 unaudited items (§6) — nothing in the audited sample suggests they are fabricated, but the brand-vs-concept defect is systematic enough that it should be assumed present until checked.

Bottom line: the catalogues are honest and unusually well-researched. Their weakness is not truthfulness but date semantics — and because the gate is "does an existing X share this defining principle", date semantics is exactly the axis that determines whether novelty scoring is sound. The fixes are small, enumerated in §3, and worth making before this ships publicly.
