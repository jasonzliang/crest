\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We state the irreducible risks plainly. \textbf{Power vs.\ multiplicity.} At $\sim$10 items per cell ($\geq$8 usable after the factual-grounding check), rigorous multiplicity correction and detecting real per-cell effects remain in tension; null/parity results are therefore hypothesis-generating, and only the largest wins are confirmatory (\S\ref{sec:experiments}). \textbf{Closed-agent reproducibility.} Frontier deep-research products drift and deprecate; their scores are time-indexed snapshots, not reproducible numbers. The reproducible core is the open-weight agent on the frozen index; closed-agent results are reported as a dated snapshot. \textbf{Frozen-index realism.} Freezing the corpus removes the live-retrieval skill that defines deep-research agents; we report both frozen and live regimes and treat neither as ``the'' answer. \textbf{Correlated judges.} Frontier judges share training data and RLHF-induced biases (verbosity, sycophancy), so panel diversity does not fully cancel them; the open-weight judge partly mitigates this. \textbf{Length.} Length may be a \emph{mediator} of creativity (elaboration), not only a confound; we report length-conditioned effects but cannot fully resolve the causal ambiguity. \textbf{Construct.} At this scale the New/Useful/Surprising factor structure is under-identified, and creativity has no ground truth---the Consensual Assessment Technique bounds, but does not eliminate, the judge-defines-construct circularity. \textbf{Conflict of interest.} One evaluated agent was built by the authors; we disclose this prominently (Ethics), exclude it from rubric design, pre-register outcome-independence, and show all findings hold with it removed.
