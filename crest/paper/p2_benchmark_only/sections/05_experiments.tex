\section{Experiments: Is Agent Creativity Axis-Dependent?}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Protocol (pre-registered).} We pre-register the per-category predictions and the analysis plan before scoring (OSF, timestamped): under a mechanistic account, agents that couple relational grounding with convergent refinement should excel at cross-domain (C3, structure-mapping \citep{gentner1983structure}) and counterfactual (C2 \citep{byrne2005rational}) synthesis, be at parity on meta-creativity (C4), and show \emph{predicted nulls} on constrained (C1) and open-ended (C5) synthesis \citep{boden2004creative,campbell1960blind}. Predictions are tested on held-out, fresh challenges.

\paragraph{Agents and fairness.} We evaluate a field of frontier deep-research agents (OpenAI, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, and open-source), each run under its \emph{provider-native web search} (no separate search key; the recommended open-source entrant is LangChain's \texttt{open\_deep\_research}) and, for the fair-run mode, over the frozen shared index---all under a matched tool/compute budget (equal search-call cap and token budget). We report a cost--creativity Pareto frontier rather than a single leaderboard rank.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Our analysis code is implemented and released. We fit per-item mixed-effects models with crossed random effects for challenge and rater, report effect sizes with bootstrap confidence intervals, and correct for multiple comparisons. Null/parity claims use \emph{equivalence testing} (TOST) with pre-registered bounds, so a non-win reads as evidence-for-equivalence rather than absence of power; the strong C2/C3 signals serve as positive controls demonstrating instrument sensitivity. Because the factual-grounding check zero-inflates the score, creativity is modeled with a two-part (hurdle) model (\S\ref{sec:grounding}); output length enters as a covariate and budgets are matched.

\paragraph{Pilot (smoke test, honest).} A small live-web pilot---5 challenges $\times$ 2 agents, scored by a 3-judge leave-one-family-out panel---validated the end-to-end pipeline and reproduced the predicted axis-dependence signal: an entrant scored creative on cross-domain (C3) but not on constrained (C1) or counterfactual (C2). We flag this as a smoke test only---one item per category and effectively one scored agent, far below the power needed for any ranking claim---so it establishes that the instrument runs, nothing more. \paragraph{The finding: a crossover, not a level shift.} The headline is not ``scores vary by task'' (expected) but a \emph{rank-reversal interaction}: the agent most creative on one axis is not the most creative on another, and cross-domain (C3) creativity dissociates from open-ended (C5) creativity across the field---there is no single creativity scalar that orders current agents.
