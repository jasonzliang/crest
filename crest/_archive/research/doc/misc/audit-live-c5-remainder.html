Citation audit: C5 remainder

Scope: C5_arts_humanities_29, C5_materials_chemistry_25, C5_biology_medicine_24, C5_engineering_28, C5_arts_humanities_49 in question/crest_challenges.audit.jsonl. Audited live-web against primary sources (publisher DOI pages, Crossref, official registries, regulator press releases, university-press books). Wikipedia was not used as the sole basis for any verdict.

No .jsonl file was edited. Replacement strings below are proposals only; each has been verified to occur EXACTLY ONCE in the raw file text.

Continuity. These five items are precisely the set that doc/misc/audit_live_C4_C5.md §6 listed as "NOT audited beyond a read-through" when its WebSearch budget ran out at 200/200, minus the four C4 items since closed by audit_live_C4_remainder.md. With this pass, claim-level live audit coverage of the 50-item file is complete. Two prior conclusions are refined here:

  • The earlier pass called catalogue quality bimodal by item ID, with the densely-cited _1x/_2x items the strong end. That is half right: C5_engineering_28 (a _2x) is indeed the most densely dated item in this batch at 95%, but its dates are the most systematically wrong of any item audited so far (§0b). Citation density was being read as a proxy for reliability; it is not one.
  • The earlier pass flagged C5_arts_humanities_49 as having a bare, undated catalogue. Confirmed and quantified: 17 of its 20 entries carry no date whatsoever, and all 20 are Western (§5.1).

Headline findings

  1. Single most exposed error, found in the largest baseline: C5_materials_chemistry_25 [5] credits metal-organic frameworks to "Yaghi/Kitagawa, late 1990s" — omitting Richard Robson, who shared the 2025 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for MOFs and whom the Nobel committee names as the originator ("It was Robson who first reasoned it would be possible to make these frameworks in the 1980s"). Robson & Hoskins published in JACS 1989/1990. A publicly released 2026 benchmark carrying this will be noticed on sight by any materials reviewer (§3.2).
  2. The open lead is confirmed and the pattern holds. C5_biology_medicine_24 dates oncolytic-virus approvals to "since 2015". The real first approvals are Latvia 2004 (Rigvir) and China 2005 (Oncorine/H101); 2015 is only the first FDA approval. Same defect as found elsewhere: prior art dated younger than reality, inflating apparent novelty.
  3. The same defect is epidemic in the two most densely dated items, and it is strictly one-directional. C5_engineering_28: 13 of 23 claim units date prior art to a brand launch or research boom rather than first publication — by up to 85 years (electroadhesion: Johnsen-Rahbek 1923, listed as "SRI ~2008") and 51 years (electropermanent magnets: US 3,089,064 filed 1959, listed as "Knaian 2010"). Only 2 of 23 survive unamended. C5_materials_chemistry_25: 13 of ~39 dated claims defective — carbon nanotubes −39 yr (Radushkevich & Lukyanovich 1952), halide perovskites −31 to −116 yr (Weber 1978; Wells 1893), amorphous metals −26, zeotypes −30, MOPs −14. Across all five items and ~130 claim units, not one date error runs in the direction that would make a submission look less novel.
  4. A defect class that the earlier passes largely missed: expert_baseline fields point graders at white space that is already occupied. (audit_live_C4_remainder.md caught one instance in C4_engineering_45; audit_live_C4_C5.md §1d checked all 20 baselines and concluded "no defects" because it tested only for dated citations.) Four more instances here, giving a base rate of 5 for 5 on items actually tested for it. The worst is C5_materials_chemistry_25, whose baseline nominates knot/entanglement order and non-atomic lattices as the winning territory — the former named and formalised by Robson himself in 1998 and instantiated by woven COF-505 which the same item lists as prior art in entry [6], the latter occupied since the Abrikosov vortex lattice of 1957. In C5_biology_medicine_24 the baseline nominates "phase behavior/condensate control, mechanical/rheological state" as the genuinely-novel territory — but condensate modulators are clinical-stage and histotripsy has FDA authorisation (Oct 2023) and Tumor Treating Fields since 2011. In C5_arts_humanities_29 the baseline's two worked examples of a "new constraint TYPE" are the defining principles of Occitan partimen / Japanese mondōka and of 4th-century Chinese huiwen grid poetry respectively. Because hard_rule R5/R3 say novelty is voided if the principle already exists, these items instruct the grader to reward what the rules require them to zero.
  5. One "right thing, wrong famous name" instance found (C5_arts_humanities_29 [15]): Agha Shahid Ali is listed as a practitioner of the "looser American ghazal" when he was its most prominent opponent, who edited Ravishing DisUnities: Real Ghazals in English (2000) specifically to insist on strict radif + qafia. Notably, the engineering item contains no name-substitution errors — its two attribution faults are an author credited on a paper he is not on (Aizenberg) and a control theorist read as a hardware paper's author (Skelton).
  6. The non-Western blind spot is real, load-bearing, and sharper than "some forms are missing." C5_arts_humanities_49's catalogue is, by inspection of all 20 forms, entirely Western (European art music plus Anglo-American popular song) — and, more diagnostically, its 20 entries are almost all thematic-return schemes. An entire axis of formal organisation is absent: form built primarily by metric/temporal transformation, which is how Javanese irama, Japanese jo-ha-kyū and Korean sanjo each organise large-scale structure (§5.2). A submission inventing "form as hierarchical metric-density expansion" collides with nothing on the list. C5_arts_humanities_29 is genuinely cross-lingual but dates its non-Western and ancient prior art too young — most sharply at [23], which credits visual/pattern poetry to Herbert (1633) when Greek technopaegnia (c. 300 BCE) and Sanskrit citrakāvya (6th-7th c. CE) precede it by 1,000-1,900 years.

0a. Why these defects are not recoverable at judge time

From judge/score.py (_judge_prompt), the judge is instructed:

"New: novelty vs the DATED KNOWN ANSWERS below. Judge ONLY against that list, NOT against the web or your own background knowledge — an idea absent from the list is new FOR THIS BENCHMARK even if you believe you have seen it, and an idea present in the list is not new even if it looks fresh."

and

"Surprising: expectation-violation vs the EXPERT BASELINE …"

Three consequences that set the severity of everything below:

  1. A missing entry is an unrecoverable false-novelty award. The judge is explicitly barred from using background knowledge to catch omitted prior art. So the MISSING-prior-art lists in this audit are not a nice-to-have — they are the only defence the scoring pipeline has. Every family listed in §1.4, §2.3, §3.5, §4.4 and §5.4 is a guaranteed New = 7-10 for an agent that rediscovers it.
  2. expert_baseline is piped in as the Surprise reference. So an expert_baseline that nominates already-occupied territory as the "genuinely surprising" answer does active harm: an agent that lands on histotripsy-like tissue mechanics, condensate control, or a partimen/huiwen-style reading-path rule collects a high Surprise score for reproducing FDA-authorised or 4th-century prior art — and the judge is forbidden from correcting it.
  3. Date errors bite indirectly rather than directly: the judge compares against list contents, but the hard_rules shown alongside it (and the agent's own prompt) reference "the dated known-answers list" and dated prior-art cutoffs, so a wrongly-young date shifts the horizon an agent must argue past. The _MAX_KNOWN_ANSWERS = 40 cap does not fire at 33.

2.2 The hard_rules and expert_baseline designate white space that is ALREADY OCCUPIED — critical

hard_rule[4] ("NEW CONSTRAINT-TYPE REQUIRED") names four constraint types as not central to any documented form: semantic/relational, inter-line logical dependency, reader-path/topological, cross-modal or generative. The expert_baseline then gives two worked examples of a winning answer: "a rule where each line must be a valid answer to the question posed by the previous line" and "a 'branching' form with two legal reading-paths whose fork is fixed by a stated rule."

Every one of these is the defining principle of a documented form — and the occupying prior art is overwhelmingly non-Western, which is exactly the blind spot the catalogue has.

3.2 The single worst finding: the MOF entry omits a 2025 Nobel laureate

Entry [5] reads "Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) — metal-node + organic-linker porous crystals (Yaghi/Kitagawa, late 1990s)". Both the attribution and the date are WRONG.

  • Richard Robson is omitted. Designed metal-organic/coordination frameworks originate with Hoskins & Robson, JACS 1989, 111, 5962 and JACS 1990, 112, 1546 — the latter titled "Design and construction of a new class of scaffolding-like materials."
  • The 2025 Nobel Prize in Chemistry went to Susumu Kitagawa, Richard Robson and Omar M. Yaghi "for the development of metal-organic frameworks" — and the Nobel committee's own account states: "It was Robson who first reasoned it would be possible to make these frameworks in the 1980s."
  • The date is ~10 years young. Timeline: Robson 1989/1990 → term "metal-organic framework" coined by Yaghi & Li, JACS 1995, 117, 10401 → Kitagawa (Kondo et al., Angew. Chem. 1997, 36, 1725) → MOF-5 (Li, Eddaoudi, O'Keeffe, Yaghi, Nature 1999, 402, 276). Earlier still: Kinoshita et al., BCSJ 1959, 32, 1221 (Cu(I)-adiponitrile 3D net).

I verified the Nobel citation and the Robson attribution independently. https://www.kva.se/en/news/the-nobel-prize-in-chemistry-2025/ ; https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/2025-chemistry-nobel-prize-goes-to-the-scientists-behind-metal-organic-frameworks/4022287.article ; https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1021/ja00160a038 ; https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1021/ja00146a033

5.2 MISSING prior art — three verified directly by me, as a floor

These three are enough on their own to establish the gate failure, independent of the fuller list:

  1. Javanese irama levels + colotomic gongan structure. Irama is a hierarchy of metric density in which each level doubles the number of elaborating strokes per balungan beat — lancar (1) → tanggung (2) → dadi (4) → wilet (8) → rangkep (16) — so a single composition can be stretched or compressed to different total lengths and different degrees of embellishment by planned irama changes (tanggung → dadi → wilet → rangkep). Orthogonally, colotomic structure defines the form: in ladrang the gong ageng marks a 32-beat gongan while the kenong subdivides every 8. A large-scale scheme for organising sectional succession by hierarchical metric-density transformation is not any of the 20 listed forms — it is not variation (the melody is fixed), not rondo/ternary (no thematic return pattern), and not moment form. Sumarsam (Wesleyan), "Temporal and Density Flow in Javanese Gamelan": https://sumarsam.faculty.wesleyan.edu/files/2023/01/4_Temporal_and_Density_Flow.pdf ; https://music.arts.uci.edu/abauer/6.2/readings/Short_primer_Javanese_Gamelan_Sri_Duhita.pdf
  2. Japanese jo-ha-kyū 序破急. A three-phase temporal-proportion principle — jo (slow, unmeasured introduction), ha (measured, accelerating development), kyū (rapid conclusion) — originating in 8th-century gagaku and generalised by Zeami into the governing structural principle of noh, applied recursively at the level of the play, the scene, and the phrase. It is a proportional/temporal scheme, not a thematic-return scheme, so no listed entry covers it. https://db2.the-noh.com/edic/2009/06/johakyu_1.html
  3. Korean sanjo. A 4-7 movement solo suite whose sections are defined and ordered by their rhythmic cycle (jangdan), progressing monotonically from the slow jinyangjo through jungmori, jungjungmori to the fast jajinmori and hwimori — i.e. the organizing principle of the whole form is a one-way acceleration through named metric cycles. Structurally distinct from every listed form. https://www.melodigging.com/genre/sanjo ; https://accesson.kr/kaa/v.2/0/54/42772 ; https://silo.tips/download/chapter-music-of-sanjo (Caveat: sources conflict on jinyangjo's metre — popular summaries say 18/8, Korean musicology generally says 24 beats in four groups of six. I could not resolve this from primary sources, so no metre figure is asserted here. Do not propagate either number without checking.)

Note the family resemblance: three separate traditions organise large-scale form primarily by metric/temporal transformation rather than by thematic return — and the catalogue's 20 entries are almost entirely thematic-return schemes. That is a whole axis of formal organisation missing from the novelty gate, not a few stray omissions.

5.6 Corrections to claims in my own audit brief (so they do not propagate)

The parallel audit caught several errors in the instructions I wrote, which is exactly what a good audit should do. Recorded here so they are not copied into the dataset:

  • jinyangjo metre: popular summaries say 18/8; Korean musicology generally says 24 beats in four groups of six. Unresolved — assert neither (see §5.2).

  • In the nūba, raṣd is a mode (ṭabʿ), not a mīzān; the five mīzān are basīṭ, qāʾim wa-niṣf, bṭāyḥī, darj, quddām. Morocco preserves 11 complete + 5 partial nūbāt (24 is the legendary al-Andalus count).
  • In the fasıl the taksim comes FIRST, not last; şarkı belongs to the 19th-c. modern fasıl. The peşrev's refrain is the mülâzime (teslim is the connecting tag).
  • "Standard pattern" is A. M. Jones's term; "time line" is Nketia's — my brief reversed them. Kubik's term is "inherent rhythm", not "resultant melody". Berliner, The Soul of Mbira, is UC Press 1978.
  • Do not attribute cycle-not-sectional essentialism to Agawu — he argues against it.
  • Agbekor's sections are ʋulolo → adzo → ʋutsotsoe; "banyinyi" is not verifiable for Agbekor (Wikipedia-only, and associated with Agbadza).
  • Uyghur märghul is an interlude inside chong näghmä, not its third section (that is mäshräp); shashmaqom talqin is a piece, not a sub-cycle (those are Savt and Moghulcha).
