1. Spot-checks

I checked eight claims, chosen because each would flip my decision if false. Two reviewer claims are overturned in detail while their substance survives; three reviewer numbers are worse than the reviewer stated; one reviewer argument I discard as a weak instrument.

grep -ci canary question/crest_challenges.jsonl → 0. doc/datasheet.md:50-52 states "Each item embeds a canary string enabling post-hoc detection in future model training corpora" and datasheet.md §5 further leans on it ("not a training set (canaries + license restrict training use)"). paper/p2_benchmark_only/sections/03_benchmark_design.tex and paper/p3_caesar_hybrid/sections/03_crest_benchmark.tex both repeat the claim. The only canary hits in the repo are these prose assertions plus incidental occurrences in agent transcripts. One command, contradicts a datasheet claim. SC-5 — hard_rules self-contradiction in C1_materials_chemistry_01. HOLDS (all four sub-claims).

Read verbatim from the item:

  • Se permit/ban. R6: "X may be a halide (Cl, Br, I), a chalcogenide (S, Se), a nitride, or an oxide". R9: "(This deliberately bans Ag, Au, Pd, Pt, Rh, Ir, Re, In, Te, Se, Bi, and Sb…)". Direct contradiction.
  • R4 excludes the item's own reference answer. R4 requires mu = rB/rX in [0.40, 0.90] and explains it was "deliberately widened below the classic 0.414 radius-ratio cutoff to admit sulfide perovskites." The item's own expert_baseline reports "octahedral mu~0.39-0.42 (Shannon radii)" for BaZrS3/BaHfS3 (Zr4+ 0.72 / S2- 1.84 = 0.391). The rule written to admit sulfide perovskites excludes the sulfide perovskite the item nominates.
  • R2 permits a motif and bans its definition. R2 permits "a vacancy-ordered A2BX6" and in the same sentence requires "Corner-sharing BX6 octahedral connectivity in at least 2 dimensions … this bans 0-D/1-D isolated-octahedra motifs." Vacancy-ordered A2BX6 (Cs2SnI6-type) is the isolated-octahedra motif.
  • R11 ∧ R12 unsatisfiable for a retrieval-only agent. R11 demands "predicted energy above the convex hull … <= 50 meV/atom, with the competing decomposition products explicitly named"; R12 voids the answer if the composition "appear[s] in … ICSD/Materials Project/OQMD/AFLOW". A composition with a citable hull energy is in the database; one absent from the database has no citable hull energy. A web-research agent cannot run its own DFT.

Reviewer overturned on a citation, upheld on the finding: R2 asserts the R11/R12 catch-22 "also in C1_materials_chemistry_31." That item has 7 hard rules, no R11 or R12 — the citation FAILS. The defect is present in that item under different numbering: R1 requires checking "ICSD, Materials Project" for non-existence while R3 requires "the DFT/Materials Project band gap" and R4 the "Materials Project grand-potential/reaction phase diagram." Same catch-22, wrong rule labels.

I also confirm C3_arts_humanities_17 R5 misstates a theorem it forbids misstating: it requires correct use of "'holonomy around a contractible loop is trivial iff the connection is flat'." As written for a single loop the forward direction is false — a non-flat connection can have trivial holonomy around a particular contractible loop. The correct statement quantifies over all contractible loops.

SC-7 — hard_rules bind (R2's concession). HOLDS, and it is the artifact's best asset.

Useful when a judge cites a rule violation: 2.69 (n=221 verdicts). Without: 8.37 (n=73). Gap 5.68. R2's 2.84/8.27 replicates at the earlier n. I add a dose-response check the reviewers did not run: corr(n_hard_rules, mean Useful) = **-0.495** across 32 items — correctly signed. But the same data shows the judge cannot agree on which rule was broken: mean Jaccard on the violated-rule sets between the two judges is 0.127, with exact-set agreement 11.6%, and both judges report no violation in only 11 of 147 records. So the presence of a violation is a strong signal and its identity is noise. Both facts must be reported.

SC-8 — 12000-character truncation. HOLDS; substantially worse than R2 stated.

judge/score.py:121 — {report_text[:12000]}. Measured against the transcripts:

  • mean answer length 21,451 chars, median 17,972, max 57,282;
  • 66.0% of scored answers (97 of 147) exceed 12,000 chars;
  • for those, the judge sees a mean of 49% of the answer;
  • 56.8% of all violation citations come from records whose answer was cut.

R2 quoted ~38%; the true exposure is that two-thirds of answers are judged at roughly half length. Hard rules of the form "the answer must state every ion's oxidation state," "the specific radii used must be reported," "report the DFT band gap" are satisfied in exactly the methods/table sections that get cut. Truncation therefore manufactures violations, and a violation caps Useful at <=3, and Useful is the sole binding axis for 96 of 147 records.

1b. The objection all four reviewers missed — and it is the one that kills the leaderboard

Leave-one-family-out, with a 3-vendor panel and one severity-outlier judge, assigns a different panel to each entrant family by construction:

entrant family panel actually used
openai_shallow, openai_deepresearch openai {anthropic, google}
claude_shallow anthropic {google, openai}
gemini_shallow, gemini_deepresearch_max google {anthropic, openai} — no google

And judge_google is a severity outlier, measured within the same records (paired, so this is pure severity and not content):

judge n verdicts New Useful Surprising flags creative mean #violations
judge_anthropic 122 5.24 3.42 4.04 6.6% 2.48
judge_openai 85 5.46 2.64 4.48 7.1% 3.61
judge_google 87 7.89 6.49 8.08 55.2% 1.16

Paired within-record differences on min(N,U,S): google - openai = **+2.04**, google - anthropic = **+3.24**, anthropic - openai = **-0.02**.

So two judges are calibrated to each other and the third is ~2-3 points more lenient, and LOFO deterministically removes the lenient one exactly for the Google entrants. Consequence:

observed min-axis leaderboard        google on panel?
  openai_shallow          5.20            yes
  openai_deepresearch     5.10            yes
  claude_shallow          3.14            yes
  gemini_shallow          3.08            NO
  gemini_deepresearch_max 2.34            NO
observed spread                       = 2.86
mean(with lenient judge) - mean(without) = 1.77

About 60% of the entire observed leaderboard spread is attributable to which judge families LOFO happened to leave in. The two bottom-ranked entrants are precisely the two never scored by the lenient judge, and the design provides no common-judge comparison for them at all — judge_google never scores a Google entrant, so there is no judge shared by all five entrants. The leaderboard is not identifiable from this data.

This also explains R3's finding more economically than R3 does: if one severity dimension dominates every axis for every entrant, of course New and Surprising produce the same ranking.

The anti-self-preference control (O7) has, at k=2, introduced a larger bias than the one it removes. The fix is cheap (§4, item 1) — but nothing in either paper survives without it.

2. Double-counting, contradictions, and root causes

Four reviewers found four field defects. There are four root causes, and they do not map one-to-one onto the four fields.

RC-A — One 12-character instrument bug drives most of the "field" objections

report_text[:12000] truncates 66% of answers to ~49%. Truncation manufactures hard-rule violations. A violation caps Useful at <=3. Useful is the sole binding axis in 96 of 147 records. Therefore:

  • R2's "the rules are unverifiable by construction" is partly a truncation artifact, not a rule-authoring artifact;
  • R3's "Useful ICC(1,1) = 0.184, the worst axis" is downstream of judges disagreeing about content half of them cannot see;
  • R4's "axis_isolated has negative discriminative validity" is entirely downstream. My measurement of the labeled-axis hit rate is not the flat 28% R4 reports — it is violently heterogeneous: C1 (labeled usefulness) 82%, C2 (surprise) 41%, C3 (novelty) 4%, C5 (novelty) 3%, overall 35% vs 33% chance. axis_isolated is not an invalid label. It is a valid label for C1 and a label that Useful-domination overwrites for C3/C5. Fix RC-A and this objection has to be re-measured from scratch.

Direct contradiction between R2 and R4. R2's positive finding (hard_rules demonstrably bind, 5.68-point gap) is the mechanism of R4's negative finding (the labeled axis is not the binding axis). They cannot both be reported as independent defects. R4's axis_isolated objection should be withdrawn as an independent charge and folded into RC-A.

RC-B — New and Surprising are two names for one measurement against one author-written key

known_answers is a negative key ("do not credit this"). expert_baseline is a positive key that in C2 literally enumerates the intended surprises. Both are pasted into the same prompt. So both axes ask the judge one question: is this content on the author's list? Hence r=0.93, disattenuated 3.11, identical rankings, kappa 0.135.

R1's "the novelty yardstick is uncitable" and R3's "Surprising is redundant" and R3's "graded recall against an author key" are one defect with two field names. Any remediation that touches only known_answers or only expert_baseline will not move the correlation.

The authors already know this. doc/misc/field_justification.md reports "CREST's own judges score New and Surprising at r = 0.77-0.94, with 28% of verdicts assigning identical values," cites the CFA literature showing two-factor beats three-factor, and recommends demoting Surprising to secondary. The finding is in the repo and not in the papers.

RC-C — Documentation-as-claim: the papers describe an instrument that was never built

One defect, eight instances, spread across all four reviews: canaries (SC-1), frozen index (SC-2), rubric.md not loaded and its key clause dropped (SC-3), randomize_answer_order: true read by nothing (SC-3), grounding_gate.metric: fact_precision read by nothing (SC-3), --prereg pointing at a nonexistent file that is never consumed (SC-3), OSF timestamp (SC-4), human validation (13 assertion sites, zero data). schema.json belongs here too: it is a well-formed JSON Schema that no code validates — grep -rn "jsonschema" --include=*.py . returns nothing — so its required list, including known_answers, is decorative, and Challenge.from_json (run_crest.py:35-38) uses d.get(), so a missing known_answers becomes None and the judge silently reads "(none provided)".

This is not four field problems. It is one process problem: specification documents are being shipped as evidence. For a D&B submission that is the single most reputationally expensive category of error, because every instance is falsifiable in one command.

RC-D — Under-powered by construction, and the project's own doc says so

R3's rater-level unreliability and R4's item-level power complaint are the same complaint at two levels of the design.

  • Rater level: ICC(1) = 0.395/0.184/0.226; deployed panel k=2 gives ICC(2) = 0.566/0.311/0.369; the project's own benchmark_best_practices.md:125 bar of ICC(k)>=0.80 needs 6/18/14 judges.
  • Item level: MDE for a paired agent contrast at n=10 items/cell is 2.47 points (R4: 2.26); the observed C2 spread — p2's declared positive control — is 1.75 (R4: 1.83). Below MDE. category x domain cells are n=1 (10 cells) or n=2 (20 cells), so the (1|challenge)+(1|judge) model with a domain term promised in p2/05_experiments.tex:8 is not identifiable.
  • ICC(item) is far lower than R4 reported: 0.171 New / 0.041 Useful / 0.006 min3 (R4 said 0.359). Items explain essentially no variance in the compound endpoint. R4 understated this.

benchmark_best_practices.md prescribes the remedies — size the panel by Spearman-Brown, use min(New, Useful, Surprising) on the 1-10 scale instead of a "fragile threshold crossing", report ICC(k) as headline, encode checkable constraints as a "deterministic pre-filter before human rating." None of the four was done. The most efficient rebuttal the authors can write is one that follows their own document.

Reviewer arguments I discard

R1's dose-response argument (corr(n known_answers, mean New) = +0.51). I measure +0.341 on 32 items, so the sign replicates and the magnitude does not. But the argument is a weak instrument regardless: list length is not a dose of novelty difficulty. Authors wrote longer lists for well-trodden areas, which are also the areas where a judge with no anchor rates generously. The positive correlation is as consistent with "authors calibrated list length to field maturity" as with "the field does not work." R1 should drop this and lead with the zero-citation finding, which is unarguable.

R1's "22 items in 11 wordings" / "7 items with obviousness-tags." Both undercount the heterogeneity. With a combined pattern I find 20 of 50 items carry judge-directive prose inside known_answers, 38 entries, and 27 distinct normalized wordings — not 11. And 11 items (not 7) contain obviousness-tags rather than prior art. Crucially the split is systematic by category, which R1 missed: for C1/C3/C5 known_answers holds published prior art; for C2 it holds the author's list of "obvious" counterfactual consequences — e.g. C2_law_policy_37: "Dockets overwhelmed and huge backlogs form without negotiated pleas (obvious)." There is no publication asserting that. So known_answers is not one field measuring one construct noisily; it is two constructs sharing a key, and the C2 half cannot carry a citation even in principle. That reframing matters for remediation: adding DOIs fixes C1/C3/C5 and is impossible for C2, which needs a separate field.

R1's zero-URL/zero-DOI count and missing prior art. Confirmed: 729 entries, 0 containing http, 0 containing a DOI, 50.6% with no 4-digit year (R1: 52.6%). And C5_law_policy_47 has 21 entries covering FPTP through liquid democracy while omitting Phragmén (1894), Nanson (1882), and random ballot / random dictatorship (Gibbard 1977) — three methods whose defining principles are absent from all 21 listed entries. An agent that proposes sortition-over-ballots earns New credit for a 1977 result. Upheld.

3. Triage: BLOCKING / MAJOR / MINOR

The line I draw. For a D&B submission the reviewer's decision turns on two things: (i) does the released artifact match its description, and (ii) is the headline claim identifiable from the released data. So:

  • BLOCKING = the submitted claim cannot be made true within a rebuttal cycle, either because it needs new data collection or because making it true requires withdrawing the paper's contribution. Note that "the fix is cheap" does not make an objection non-blocking if the fix changes every number in the paper — a rebuttal cannot introduce an entirely new results section.
  • MAJOR = repairable before camera-ready with existing data plus engineering, or removable by an honest text edit that leaves a contribution standing.
  • MINOR = hygiene. A reviewer will note it and it will not move a score.

BLOCKING

B1. The leaderboard is confounded with panel composition (§1b). 1.77 of the 2.86-point observed spread tracks whether the lenient judge was on the panel; no judge is shared by all five entrants. Every ranking, dissociation, and crossover claim in both papers is unidentifiable. The re-judge that fixes it is nearly free, but it replaces the entire results section — that is a revision, not a rebuttal.

B2. "Human-validated judge panel" with zero human data. Asserted in 13 places including both abstracts, p2 §01's contribution list, all of p2 §04, both conclusions, and datasheet.md. find . -iname "*human*" -o -iname "*annot*" -o -iname "*rater*" returns nothing. p2 §04 goes furthest: it reports a "bounded scope (honest)" result that the panel "is reliable on C2/C3/C4 but not on C1/C5" and builds a validity-stratification protocol on it. That stratification is derived from a study that does not exist. Credible expert annotation cannot be collected in a rebuttal window, and for a benchmark whose entire instrument is a subjective LLM judge with ICC(k=2)=0.31 on the binding axis, an unvalidated judge is a fundamental artifact deficiency, not a missing appendix.

B3. The three-axis construct is not identified (RC-B). "The first benchmark to score deep-research outputs on the full New/Useful/Surprising triad" is p3's stated contribution. A disattenuated New-Surprising correlation of 3.11, Surprising removing 2 of 42 records from an N&U gate, and 9 of 10 C2 items handing the judge the answer key together mean the triad is a dyad plus a leak. Repairing it means rewriting expert_baseline for 50 items and re-measuring. The paper can retreat to a two-axis claim — which doc/misc/field_justification.md already recommends — but the triad claim as submitted cannot be rescued.

B4. Pre-registration misrepresentation (SC-4). git log -S caesar_win plus two git show commands. Enum named after the intended winner; renamed 14 hours pre-submission in a commit titled "honesty fixes"; no commit with predictions and no results; "(OSF, timestamped)" with no OSF record; --prereg pointing at a file that does not exist and is never read. The only repair is to delete every pre-registration claim — which removes p3's sole defense against "this is post hoc," i.e. removes the argument the paper is built on. Unrecoverable this cycle.

MAJOR

M1. 12000-char truncation (SC-8, RC-A). Judge-only re-run. Must be first; every other number depends on it.

M2. Self-contradictory and unsatisfiable hard rules (SC-5). At least 1 item verified in full detail with four independent defects; R2 reports >=6 of 50. Needs a rule audit plus re-judge. Bounded work.

M3. expert_baseline leaks target scores (2 items) and answer keys (all C2). Data edit plus re-judge. Cheap and high-value.

M4. Asserted-absent artifacts other than human data: canaries, order randomization, fact_precision gate, dead config keys, unvalidated schema.json. Canaries and order randomization are each <20 lines — implement them. The frozen index is not; delete the claim.

M5. threshold_all_three = 6.0 is unpre-registered and fragile (27.2% at 6.0; 5.4%-36.1% across +/-1; 25.9% of records sit exactly at 6.00). Switch the primary endpoint to min(N,U,S) on the 1-10 scale, per the project's own benchmark_best_practices.md. Analysis-only.

M6. Schema and loader hygiene: schema.json never validated, additionalProperties unset, no enum on domain, no minItems on known_answers, category_name absent from the schema and silently dropped by from_json and present in 6 distinct values (one item carries a long-form description as its value), hard_rules not in required, time_sensitivity not written to records.jsonl, d.get() turning a missing known_answers into "(none provided)". Half a day, no re-run, plus a CI test.

M7. Statistical over-reach: drop domain from the mixed model (cells n=1-2, not identifiable); report the MDE; state that C2 fails as a positive control at 1.75 < 2.47. Text plus re-analysis.

MINOR

  • time_sensitivity has post_cutoff n=1 and is never written to records — drop the field or populate it. It currently supports no contamination analysis at all.
  • 50.6% of known_answers entries lack a year despite the field's own "dated" framing.
  • rationale is 20.3% of item bytes and is never read at runtime (the authors already document this).
  • With k=2 after LOFO, "median-aggregated" is a mean of two. Reword.

4. Prioritized remediation plan

Ordered by (value / effort), and by dependency — item 1 must precede all analysis because every number changes.

# Change Kills Effort Needs
1 Re-judge everything with a common panel and no truncation. Delete [:12000] (chunk-and-aggregate, or a long-context judge). 5. Honest minimum viable claim, today, with no new data

What this artifact can support

  1. A released benchmark artifact. 50 expert-authored deep-research creativity challenges with per-item scoring machinery and an open judge harness, agent-facing prompts held short and open (median ~110 words) with the scoring machinery judge-side. That design choice is real, defensible, and novel. This is a genuine D&B contribution and it is not what the reviewers attacked.

  2. One positive empirical finding: hard_rules bind. Useful is 2.69 when a judge cites a rule violation vs 8.37 when it does not (n=294 verdicts), and corr(n_hard_rules, mean Useful) = -0.50 across 32 items. This is the artifact's only correctly-signed dose-response, and — importantly — it is a within-verdict contrast, so it survives the panel-composition confound that destroys the leaderboard. Report it with its limit: judges agree a rule was broken but not which one (Jaccard 0.127).

  3. A publishable negative/methodological finding. On 147 verdicts: New and Surprising are not separable (per-judge r=0.93; disattenuated correlation 3.11, a model violation); single-rater ICC(1) is 0.40/0.18/0.23 so the deployed 2-judge panel reaches ICC(2) of 0.57/0.31/0.37; the binding axis is Useful in 96 of 147 records; and — the sharpest result — leave-one-family-out with a 3-vendor panel makes the leaderboard unidentifiable, because judge severity varies by 2-3 points on the 1-10 scale and LOFO assigns panels by entrant family. That last result is a real, generalizable warning to everyone building LLM-judge leaderboards with self-preference controls, and CREST is in a strong position to be the paper that reports it.

A paper making claims 1-3 is honest, is supported by data in this repo today, and is interesting. It is a different paper from either submission.

6. AC summary

The four reviewers are substantially right and somewhat over-counted. Of eight decision-relevant claims I checked, six HOLD, two are PARTIAL (R3's tau=1.00 and sole-binding-axis framing; R2's C1_31 citation), and in four cases the true state is worse than the reviewer reported (truncation 66% not 38%; disattenuated r 3.11 not ">1"; ICC(item) 0.006-0.18 not 0.359; two score-leaking items not one). One reviewer argument — R1's dose-response — I discard as a weak instrument. One reviewer charge — R4's axis_isolated invalidity — I fold into another root cause and it must be re-measured after the truncation fix.

More importantly: all four reviewers missed the objection that actually decides the paper. Leave-one-family-out with a 3-vendor panel and a 2-3 point judge-severity spread makes the leaderboard non-identifiable, and accounts for roughly 60% of the observed agent spread. The four field-schema critiques are about whether the axes mean anything; this one is about whether the headline number means anything. It does not.

The good news, and the authors should hear it: the single highest-value fix costs hours, not weeks. A judge-only re-run with a common panel and no truncation (a generation cache exists) plus a day of deleting unsupported claims removes every objection a reviewer can verify in one command. What remains after that is a real artifact and an honest, genuinely interesting methodological result about the limits of LLM-judged creativity. That paper is publishable. Neither of the papers currently in front of me is.
