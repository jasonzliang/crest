NEW — has this material been written down before?

For each answer, break it into its building blocks: the claims, mechanisms, or
mappings it relies on. Take the block you are least able to place. Name up to two
specific places you believe that block already appears: a named work, a paper,

For New, an answer is stronger when its least placeable building block is harder
to place specifically. Distinguish a block placeable only in a subfield or genre

Never judge the combination for New: if you can name sources for every block,

USEFUL — does the idea actually work?

Take each answer's central proposal. State its mechanism, whether that mechanism
is worked out or merely asserted, and whether the claimed payoff follows.
Requirements the question explicitly states count here too. Do not judge novelty.

For Useful, consider how well the proposal works and meets stated requirements:
worked out well enough to act on with a supported payoff; coherent with minor
gaps or unstated assumptions; in need of repair because its mechanism is asserted,
payoff unsupported, or a requirement unmet; or inconsistent, off-topic, or without
substance. Explain the comparison using the mechanisms and claimed payoffs.

Invention is what these questions ask for. A coined concept, hypothetical
mechanism, or invented example offered as a proposal is the thing being evaluated,
not an error. Factual accuracy applies to statements presented as established
fact: a citation, statistic, result, attribution, or historical event. A
load-bearing false factual claim makes the proposal in need of repair or worse
under Useful. Judge New and Surprising independently of that factual error.


SURPRISING — did the answer go where you would have expected?

State in one clause the obvious approach to this question: the direction a
competent answer would most likely take. Compare how far each answer departs
from that direction. For Surprising, consider which direction is more unexpected,
considering whether the departure is central to its main point or incidental.

An unpredictable central move is a stronger departure than an unpredictable
incidental detail. Developing the expected direction further or combining it
in a way the question did not ask for departs more than taking it directly.
Reaching outside the question is one way to depart, not the only one: an answer
can stay entirely within the question's terms and defy expectation, for example
by showing that the obvious approach fails. Judge direction independently of
correctness; correctness belongs to Useful.
