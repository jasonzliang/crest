\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Deep-research agents have been judged only on correctness. We introduced \textbf{CREST}, the first
benchmark to ask whether their outputs are \emph{creative}---New, Useful, and Surprising---and used
it to establish a task-dependent effect: web-based exploration makes a research agent more creative
on cross-domain and counterfactual tasks, but not on constrained or open-ended ones, with ablation
evidence that specific components cause specific gains and with the creative reports remaining
well-sourced. The mixed win/loss pattern, on a benchmark whose own author's system loses 2 of 5
task types, is what makes the finding credible rather than self-serving. We release CREST---
questions, frozen retrieval snapshot, human-validated judge, and analysis code---so the community
can measure, and improve, the creativity of research agents, not just their accuracy.
