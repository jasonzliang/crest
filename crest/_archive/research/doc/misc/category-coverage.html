Do we need more categories? — coverage analysis

Short answer: No. The 5 categories are theory-complete for creative process, and on a hard double-check (below) even the strongest candidate — Scientific Hypothesis Generation — does not clearly earn a 6th category. Stay at 5 and scale to 50.

Double-check (revised): is C6 Scientific Hypothesis Generation really a distinct category?

Verdict: defensible as a task, weak as a distinct category for THIS taxonomy — do not add it.

  1. Process redundancy. CREST's categories are creative processes; abductive hypothesis generation overlaps C3 (recombine distant evidence), C1 (constrained invention), and C4 (product-type "invent an X"). It introduces no new mechanism, and the finding is about mechanism dissociation.
  2. Likely non-dissociation from C3. Its predicted mechanism and direction pattern with C3 (favored), so it adds breadth but little discriminating power — and risks failing CREST's own discriminant-validity bar (the thing that makes the taxonomy credible).
  3. Harder to score validly. "Useful hypothesis" = testable + plausible + explanatory (contested, expert-dependent), and bounding "novel hypothesis" against a dated known-answers list is very hard (huge hypothesis space). Steelman for adding it: C4 ("invent a method") is already a product-type category, so C6 ("invent a hypothesis") is structurally parallel, and it is the single most on-mission task for a research agent. But that is a use-case argument, not a process argument — and mixing a use-case axis into a process taxonomy is exactly the inconsistency to avoid. Represent hypothesis-generation as domain instances inside C1/C5 instead.

What the 5 categories are, and why they are principled

CREST's categories are not arbitrary; each instantiates a distinct creative process from the literature, and together they were chosen so an agent's creativity can dissociate across them (the dissociation is the paper's finding).

CREST Creative process Theory anchor Isolated axis
C1 Constrained exploratory (search a fixed space) Boden 2004; Simonton 2003 Useful
C2 Counterfactual reasoning over a causal model Byrne 2005 (rational imagination) Surprising
C3 Cross-Domain combinational (structure-mapping) Gentner 1983; Falkenhainer 1989 New
C4 Meta-Creativity reflective / space-altering Boden (transformational, meta) all three
C5 Open-Ended transformational / blind variation Boden 2004; Campbell 1960 New

Coverage check against the major creativity taxonomies

  • Boden's trichotomy (combinational / exploratory / transformational): fully covered (C3 / C1 / C5). ✓
  • Rhodes' 4 P's (Person, Process, Product, Press): CREST is a Process×Product benchmark; Person and Press don't apply to an agent. ✓ (correctly out of scope)
  • Guilford / Torrance divergent thinking (fluency, flexibility, originality, elaboration): CREST measures originality (New) well, but does not measure fluency/flexibility — the ability to generate many, diverse valid ideas. This is a genuine gap, but filling it needs a set-level scoring protocol (score a set of ideas on quantity × diversity × quality, à la NoveltyBench/Torrance), not the single-best per-item scoring CREST uses. → v2 extension, not a drop-in category.
  • Getzels & Csikszentmihalyi problem-finding: CREST tests creative problem-solving, not problem-finding (posing novel questions). Distinct and research-relevant. → candidate.
  • Scientific creativity / hypothesis generation (Klahr & Dunbar; Simonton): generating a novel, testable, non-obvious hypothesis from evidence. C4 is method-invention and C2 is counterfactual reasoning — neither is hypothesis generation. → strongest candidate, because it is precisely what a research agent is for.

Recommendation

  1. Keep the 5 as the core that carries the finding. They are complete over creative process and each is theory-grounded; adding categories dilutes per-cell power (already thin at ~6/cell) and invites scope creep. For the task-dependence claim, 5 is right.
  2. Do not add C6 as a category (see the double-check above): it is redundant in process, likely non-dissociating from C3, and hard to score validly. If hypothesis-generation coverage is wanted, add it as domain instances within C1 (constrained) and C5 (open-ended), not a 6th category.
  3. Defer C7 Divergent Fluency/Flexibility to v2. Valuable, but it requires a different (set-level) scoring model; bolting it onto the single-best rubric would weaken construct validity.
  4. Do not confuse domains with categories. CREST already spans 6 domains (bio/med, materials, econ, law, engineering, arts) — that is breadth. Categories are process types. Adding domains ≠ adding categories.

Interaction with the count question — SETTLED

The scale-up recommended below happened: the shipped set is 5 categories × 10 items = 50, and C6 was not added. This section is retained as the rationale for the current taxonomy, not as an open decision. (The count audit it originally cross-referenced, question_audit.md, was a review of the earlier 30-item set and has been deleted as spent; doc/item_vetting.md and doc/misc/audit_live_*.md are the per-item record for the shipped 50.)

One-line verdict

Five categories are sufficient and defensible; the taxonomy stayed at 5 and the budget went to 10 items/category (50 total) rather than a 6th category.

Refs: Boden 2004; Gentner 1983; Falkenhainer 1989; Byrne 2005; Simonton 2003; Campbell 1960; Guilford 1967 / Torrance 1974 (divergent thinking); Rhodes 1961 (4 P's); Getzels & Csikszentmihalyi 1976 (problem-finding); Klahr & Dunbar 1988 (scientific discovery).
