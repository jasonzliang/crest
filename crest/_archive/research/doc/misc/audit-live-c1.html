Citation audit: C1

Scope: the 10 items with id prefix C1_ in question/crest_challenges.audit.jsonl. Fields audited: known_answers (highest weight — it is the novelty yardstick), expert_baseline, query. Date of audit: 2026-07-30. No .jsonl file was modified.

Method. WebSearch (until session budget exhausted at 200 calls), then WebFetch and direct HTTPS fetches against primary sources and literature APIs (Europe PMC REST, Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, PubChem, USENIX/NeurIPS proceedings, publisher and agency pages). Unfalsifiable prose, scoring-rubric language, and textbook common knowledge (Vickrey 1961, 12-TET, "villanelle is 19 lines") were not re-litigated.

Headline

Zero fabrications. Every paper, compound, statute, standard, mission and product named across all 10 items exists. That is the single most important result: the C1 prior-art lists were built from real literature, not hallucinated.

5 WRONG, 9 IMPRECISE, 2 UNVERIFIABLE, ~80 CONFIRMED. Of the 5 WRONG, 3 sit inside known_answers and are the ones that actually matter:

  1. C1_biology_medicine_00 — "dynobactin N" does not exist; the compound is dynobactin A.
  2. C1_biology_medicine_00 — "RG6114" is not the LepB inhibitor G0775. RG6114 is inavolisib (GDC-0077 / Itovebi), a PI3Kα inhibitor for breast cancer. Wrong drug entirely.
  3. C1_materials_chemistry_31 — LAGP is given the LATP formula, erasing the germanium dependence that the item's own baseline argument turns on.

The other 2 WRONG are both inside a single citation in C1_engineering_04 (a phantom co-author and a wrong companion journal).

The four prior-pass corrections: 3 of 4 fully re-verify (AWS Spot timeline + <5% interruption; Rao/Burgess/Kaffine PNAS volume-issue-pages; FCC 22-74 date). The fourth (Cs2TiI6 band gap) is half-corroborated — the 1.56 eV direct figure holds, the 1.58 eV indirect figure does not reproduce from any source I could reach.

Overall assessment: are C1's novelty baselines trustworthy enough to score against?

Yes, after the four known_answers fixes (items 1, 2, 4 above plus the engineering citation).

The reasoning:

  1. Nothing is fabricated. Across ~95 checkable claims spanning ten disciplines, every named paper, compound, statute, standard, mission and commercial product turned out to exist. This is the property that matters most for a novelty yardstick: a hallucinated prior-art entry would penalise a genuinely novel answer for "restating" something that was never published. That failure mode is absent from C1.

  2. The error rate is low and the errors are shallow. 5 WRONG out of ~95 (~5%), and four of the five are labelling errors — a wrong compound suffix, a wrong internal drug code, a phantom co-author, a wrong journal. In every one of those cases the underlying target/mechanism/prior art is correctly identified, so the novelty boundary the entry draws is still in the right place. An agent proposing BamA would still be correctly marked not-new; an agent proposing LepB would still be correctly marked not-new.

  3. One error has real semantic bite. C1_materials_chemistry_31's LAGP formula is the exception. That item's whole novelty framing is "you can't get >1 mS/cm plus Li-metal stability without sulfides or Ge/rare-earth oxides," and the prior-art list as written contains no germanium NASICON at all — LAGP is spelled with the titanium formula. An answer that proposed a Ge-free NASICON could be scored as more novel than it is, or a judge could get confused about what the excluded-element constraint even covers. Fix this one before release.

  4. The expert_baseline fields are in better shape than expected. These are prose arguments, mostly unfalsifiable by design, but where they make hard checkable claims they hold up: Bartel τ < 4.18 is verbatim correct, MurJ's structural and superfamily characterisation is correct, the OST/Liability-Convention article mapping is verbatim correct, and the TPU material data is right. The two soft spots (mixed crustal-abundance datasets, the unverified murJ Tn-seq citation) affect the decoration of the argument, not its logic.

  5. Three items are fully clean: C1_arts_humanities_05, C1_arts_humanities_33, and C1_engineering_32. The two humanities items are notably strong — the sestina permutation, the Queneau-number criterion, the dróttkvætt line/syllable count, and the Cisiojanus dating all check out exactly, which is not the norm for humanities prior-art lists in AI-authored benchmarks.

  6. Residual risk to be aware of. (a) The IMPRECISE cluster in C1_law_policy_03 and C1_materials_chemistry_01 consists of dates and numbers that a strong answer might itself get more right than the baseline does — a judge scoring "agrees with known_answers" rather than "agrees with reality" would punish accuracy. Worth a rubric note. (b) Two items (C1_law_policy_03, C1_economics_business_02) reference fast-moving operational facts (TraCSS funding, cloud spot-market terms) that will drift; they need a re-check on any future release, not a fix now.

Verdict: release-ready for 6 of 10 items as-is, and for all 10 after applying the four WRONG fixes. The nine IMPRECISE fixes are strongly recommended but not blocking.
