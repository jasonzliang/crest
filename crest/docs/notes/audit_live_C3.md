# Live-Web Citation Audit — CREST category C3 (Cross-Domain Synthesis)

**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Scope:** all 10 items with `id` prefix `C3_` in `questions/crest_challenges.audit.jsonl` (working copy).
**No `.jsonl` file was modified.**

**Two claim classes audited:**
- **Class A — Mathematical correctness.** Is the imported formalism stated correctly? A Class A error is more serious than a citation slip because it makes the item incoherent: hard rules that mis-state the source mathematics *penalise correct answers*.
- **Class B — Prior-art / citation accuracy.** Do the named authors/papers/years/venues in `known_answers` exist and say what is claimed? A non-existent entry wrongly *denies* novelty credit; a missing entry wrongly *grants* it.

## Method and coverage caveat

Live verification used WebSearch, WebFetch, and direct API queries against Crossref, OpenAlex, arXiv, Semantic Scholar and OpenLibrary. **The session-wide WebSearch quota (200/200) was exhausted partway through** — the same wall that stopped the prior offline audit. Everything after that point was verified via WebFetch of primary/publisher pages plus the Crossref / OpenAlex / arXiv REST APIs, which is *stronger* evidence than search-result snippets. Negative ("nothing exists") claims were tested with structured queries against arXiv full-text and OpenAlex `fulltext.search` in addition to web search; those are recorded below with the queries run, since negative claims cannot be proven, only stress-tested.

`C3_law_policy_15` — flagged by the prior audit as unverified — was audited first and in full. **All 13 of its prior-art entries now verify.**

## 4. MATHEMATICAL ERRORS (Class A) — separate list

### Severity 1 — errors that make hard rules self-contradictory

**M0. `C3_engineering_16`: μ/s is stated as a mutation *load*. It is a *frequency*. The item's own gloss then contradicts itself.** ← *worst Class A error in C3*

The item asserts "equilibrium defect/debt **load** ≈ (defect-injection rate)/(review-catch strength)", i.e. load ≈ μ/s. Population genetics distinguishes two quantities that the item fuses:
- the equilibrium **frequency** of a deleterious allele at mutation–selection balance is **μ/s** (haploid/additive), or **√(μ/s)** if fully recessive;
- the equilibrium **load** — the reduction in mean fitness — is, by the **Haldane–Muller principle**, **μ** (or 2μ for a diploid, semi-dominant locus), and is **independent of s**.

The item then adds the editorial gloss that debt "cannot be driven to zero by effort on the numerator alone." Under the *load* reading this is exactly backwards: load ∝ μ only, so numerator effort is the *only* thing that works and s is irrelevant. Under the *frequency* reading μ/s is right but it is not a load. **The row is internally inconsistent on either reading**, and it appears **twice** — in `expert_baseline` item (1) and in `known_answers[5]`. A solver who correctly derives Haldane–Muller is graded *against* the rubric. Fix per §3 replacement 39.
Sources: Haldane, "The Effect of Variation of Fitness", *Am. Nat.* 71:337–349 (1937), https://doi.org/10.1086/280722 · Muller, "Our load of mutations", *Am. J. Hum. Genet.* 2:111–176 (1950) · Agrawal & Whitlock, *Annu. Rev. Ecol. Evol. Syst.* 43:115–135 (2012), https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-110411-160257

**M1. `C3_economics_business_14`: writhe is presented as a knot invariant. It is not.**
The query lists writhe among "invariants such as linking number, writhe, unknotting number and a knot polynomial", and the expert_baseline proposes "writhe / crossing-sign sum" as the high-scoring move. But writhe changes by ±1 under Reidemeister I, so it is a *regular-isotopy* invariant of a diagram only. Hard rule R2 demands "The quantity claimed to correspond to a knot invariant must be argued to be unchanged under ALL THREE 'Reidemeister' corporate operations." **The item's own suggested best answer therefore fails the item's own hard rule.** A knot-theory-literate solver who points this out will be marked down. Fix: relabel writhe explicitly as a diagram-level, R1-sensitive quantity — which is actually *more* interesting economically (it makes writhe a gauge-dependent "layering" signal rather than an invariant), but the item must say so.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writhe

**M2. `C3_economics_business_14`, R4: unknotting number defined as edge removals.**
The unknotting number is the minimum number of **crossing switches** (passing the strand through itself), not deletions. R4 prescribes the wrong source-side definition and then *asks* the solver to note that it may not be "a genuine 3-dimensional unknotting number" — so the rule is half-aware of its own error but still mandates the incorrect correspondence.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unknotting_number

**M3. `C3_materials_chemistry_39`: cellular sheaves described with sheaf-on-a-space vocabulary.**
Two conflations in the query's "relational core":
- "restriction maps between **overlapping** cells" — cells of a cell complex have disjoint interiors and do not overlap; cellular-sheaf restriction maps run along the **face/incidence** relation σ ⊴ τ.
- "the **gluing/locality axiom** (compatible local sections uniquely determine a global section)" — a cellular sheaf is a functor from the face poset to Vect and is *automatically* a sheaf in the Alexandrov topology. There is no gluing axiom to impose, and nothing is glued.

This matters because the item makes "local-consistency → restriction agreement → nonzero H¹ as a certified obstruction" its central relational cluster. A solver who correctly uses the incidence formulation will appear to have departed from the prescribed structure.
Source: https://jakobhansen.org/publications/spectralsheaves.pdf (Def. 4, Def. 5, Thm 3.1); Curry, arXiv:1303.3255

**M4. `C3_arts_humanities_41`: the Cheeger inequality is stated one-sided and mixes normalizations.**
"the Cheeger inequality bounding a graph's bottleneck (conductance) by the spectral gap" — the inequality is two-sided (λ₂/2 ≤ h ≤ √(λ₂(2Δ−λ₂))), and "conductance" belongs to the *normalized* Laplacian while the item's stated Laplacian is L = D − A (whose λ₂ bounds the **edge expansion**, not conductance). Since the whole item turns on "the Cheeger coupling of geometry to dynamics", the normalization has to be pinned down — the *upper* bound (small gap ⟹ a bottleneck exists) is the direction that does the humanistic work, and it is the one the loose phrasing obscures.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expander_graph · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheeger_constant_(graph_theory)

### Severity 2 — imprecisions that could penalise a correct solver

**M5. `C3_materials_chemistry_39`: "H^k as the obstruction to gluing".** For a genuine sheaf, gluing *always* succeeds; H¹ is the obstruction to **extending/lifting** local sections to a global one, not to gluing compatible ones. Precise: H⁰ = globally consistent assignments; H¹ = obstruction class for locally-consistent data with no global extension.

**M6. `C3_arts_humanities_17`: "holonomy around a contractible loop is trivial iff the connection is flat".** Needs universal quantification. Flat ⟺ the *restricted* holonomy group Hol⁰(∇) (generated by all null-homotopic loops) is trivial. A single contractible loop can have trivial holonomy in a curved connection.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrose–Singer_theorem

**M7. `C3_economics_business_14`, R3: "knot strands are undirected 1-manifolds".** Oriented knots and links are standard, and orientation is *required* for the crossing signs and linking number the item's own query invokes. The genuine mismatch is **weighting**, not orientation.

**M8. `C3_economics_business_14`: the string-diagram warning is oversimplified.** True for symmetric/plain monoidal categories; false for braided and ribbon ones, where the braiding axioms *give* Reidemeister II/III and (via Shum's theorem and Reshetikhin–Turaev) genuine knot invariants. The correct dividing line is symmetric (self-inverse braiding ⟹ crossings collapse) vs braided/ribbon.
Source: https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/ribbon+category

**M9. `C3_biology_medicine_12`: the initial-object gloss drops uniqueness.** "INITIAL object (a transition toward every downstream type)" describes a *weakly initial* object. Initial requires "precisely one morphism I → X" — and uniqueness is exactly the proof obligation the item's own hard rule ("discharge its universal property") demands. Also, the Yoneda *embedding* is listed among "universal constructions"; the relevant universal property is **representability**.

**M10. `C3_engineering_16`: "Kimura's ~2s fixation probability" is misattributed.** The u ≈ 2s result is **Haldane (1927)**, following a suggestion by Fisher (1922). Kimura's contribution is the diffusion formula u(p) = (1−e^{−4Nsp})/(1−e^{−4Ns}) from which 2s falls out in the 4Ns ≫ 1 limit. The general form is u ≈ 2s/σ² for offspring-number variance σ² (haploid Wright–Fisher σ²=1 ⟹ ≈2s; Moran σ²=2 ⟹ ≈s). This matters because the item's own hard rule requires "at least 3 independent **primary** sources on the population-genetics side" — misattributing the flagship result undercuts that rule, and a dual expert will notice.
Sources: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100015644 · https://doi.org/10.1017/S0370164600023993

**M11. `C3_engineering_16`: "neutral conventions fixing at ~1/(2Ne)".** The fixation probability of a new neutral mutant in a diploid population is **1/(2N)** with **census** N (it equals the mutant's initial frequency). Ne enters the neutral case through fixation *time* (≈4Ne generations) and standing diversity (θ = 4Neμ), not the fixation probability. "1/(2Ne)" is common shorthand but strictly wrong. Relatedly, the "Ne·s ~ 1" drift-vs-selection boundary is a legitimate order-of-magnitude statement, but the dimensionless parameter in Kimura's diploid diffusion is **4Ne·s**.

**M12. `C3_engineering_16` and `C3_materials_chemistry_13`: Fisher's fundamental theorem described as *bounding* an improvement rate.** FFT is an **exact equality** for the *partial* change in mean fitness attributable to allele-frequency change under selection (= the additive genetic variance in fitness), not a bound on total per-generation improvement — the total also carries an environmental/genetic-background deterioration term. Using it as a bound is defensible only in the restricted constant-fitness additive model, and neither item states that restriction. `C3_materials_chemistry_13` builds a whole expected-answer row on it ("bound the per-generation improvement of a high-throughput/active-learning campaign by the variance of formation energies"), so the assumption needs to be explicit.
Sources: Price, "Fisher's 'fundamental theorem' made clear", *Ann. Hum. Genet.* 36:129–140 (1972), https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-1809.1972.tb00764.x · Ewens, *Theor. Popul. Biol.* 36:167–180 (1989)

## 5. Novelty-gate integrity: missing prior art

A prior-art entry that does not exist wrongly *denies* novelty credit. **None was found.** The more dangerous failure is the opposite: prior art that *does* exist and is *missing* from `known_answers`, which wrongly *grants* credit. Four such gaps were found.

### G1 — `C3_arts_humanities_17`: the gauge/holonomy-on-semantic-space territory is occupied. **(Most serious gap in C3.)**

The item's weakest known-answer asserts that the only existing work is "Scattered metaphorical 'gauge symmetry of meaning' remarks … the invariance HEADLINE only — no connection, no curvature, no Wilson loop, no worked dictionary, no translation phenomena attached." That characterisation is **false**. The connection → transport → holonomy layer on *meaning spaces* is published, with measurable holonomy:

- **Coda, Courts, Wight & Truong, "Fiber Bundle Morphisms as a Framework for Modeling Many-to-Many Maps", arXiv:2203.08189 (2022)** — **pre-cutoff**, and the closest structural match: fiber bundles where the fiber over an input is the set of admissible outputs, motivated explicitly by *linguistic ambiguity* ("a single statement can have a range of different sentiment interpretations while at the same time many distinct statements can represent the same sentiment"). This is almost verbatim the item's expected-baseline row "fiber over a point = the set of admissible target-language renderings". https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08189
- Javidnia, "A Gauge Theory of Superposition: Toward a Sheaf-Theoretic Atlas of Neural Representations", arXiv:2603.00824 (Feb 2026) — discrete gauge theory over a stratified context complex, with "(O3) nontrivial holonomy (path-dependent transport around loops)" as one of three *measurable* obstructions to global interpretability. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00824
- Javidnia, "Semantic Sections: An Atlas-Native Feature Ontology for Obstructed Representation Spaces", arXiv:2603.20867 (Mar 2026) — "twisted sections capturing locally coherent but **holonomy-obstructed meanings**"; cycle consistency as the criterion for globalization. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20867
- Richards, "Do Active SAE Feature Planes Carry More Holonomy?", arXiv:2607.20522 (Jul 2026) — preregistered empirical holonomy measurement on semantic feature planes. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20522

**Impact:** an agent that produces "meaning is a section of a bundle; untranslatability is holonomy; back-translation is a Wilson loop" would currently score New credit even though the connection/transport/holonomy layer on semantic space is prior art. The *interlingual-translation* target remains unclaimed, which is what the gate should be narrowed to. Suggested addition to `known_answers`:

> `Fiber-bundle and gauge/holonomy treatments of SEMANTIC REPRESENTATION spaces: Coda, Courts, Wight & Truong, 'Fiber Bundle Morphisms as a Framework for Modeling Many-to-Many Maps' (arXiv:2203.08189, 2022) — fiber over an input = set of admissible outputs, motivated by linguistic ambiguity; and the gauge/sheaf-atlas line on LLM representations with explicitly measured HOLONOMY as an obstruction to global feature consistency (Javidnia, arXiv:2603.00824 and arXiv:2603.20867, 2026; Richards, arXiv:2607.20522, 2026). The connection -> parallel-transport -> holonomy-obstruction layer on MEANING is therefore KNOWN. New credit requires the INTERLINGUAL TRANSLATION target specifically: a gauge group of target-language renderings, back-translation as a Wilson loop, and untranslatability derived as nonzero curvature -- none of which these works address.`

### G2 — `C3_materials_chemistry_39`: sheaf Laplacians are already applied to molecular structure.

The gate holds for *crystallography, grain boundaries, and materials structure* (arXiv full-text and a full sweep of all `"cellular sheaf" | "sheaf Laplacian"` works returned nothing). But two adjacent literatures are occupied and a referee will raise them:

- **Persistent sheaf Laplacians on molecules**: Wei & Wei, "Persistent sheaf Laplacians", *Foundations of Data Science* (2024), DOI 10.3934/fods.2024033, arXiv:2112.10906 — §4.2 is titled "Complex molecules"; sheaves over atomic point clouds fusing geometry with non-geometric physical properties (atomic partial charges). Follow-ons: Hayes et al., *J. Phys. Chem. B* (2025) DOI 10.1021/acs.jpcb.5c01287; Hayes et al., *PCCP* (2026) DOI 10.1039/d5cp04098a; Ren et al., arXiv:2601.12219.
- **Sheaf cohomology on the Brillouin zone**: Zafiris & von Müller, "Electron Beams on the Brillouin Zone: A Cohomological Approach via Sheaves of Fourier Algebras", *Universe* 9(9):392 (2023), DOI 10.3390/universe9090392 — directly adjacent to the item's own Bloch-bundle known-answer.

**Impact:** moderate. The gate is defensible but under-hedged; "sheaf Laplacian applied to a chemical system" is not novel. Narrow the gate to *cellular sheaves indexed on the cell / grain / defect structure of a material*, and cite-and-distinguish these two lines.

### G3 — `C3_law_policy_40`: feedback-routing in legal case flow is already claimed.

The item's target list includes "appellate remand feedback", and its novelty gate implies queueing-with-feedback has not been applied to legal structures. But **Blumstein & Larson, "Models of a Total Criminal Justice System", *Operations Research* 17(2):199–232 (1969)** is an open flow/network model of criminal-justice case flow *with recidivism re-entry feedback*, and it seeded the JUSSIM lineage. Bakshi, Kim & Randhawa (*M&SOM* 2024) also model within-case re-entry (adjournments, multiple hearings).

**Impact:** low-to-moderate, and easily repaired. The item's *strongest* surviving novelty claim is untouched: no paper anywhere applies Kingman / heavy-traffic G/G/1 **variability** analysis to court delay, remand, or precedent propagation (Mukherjee & Whalen 2018 use a time-varying priority queue with heavy-tailed disposal times but not c_a²+c_s²; Bakshi et al. 2024 explicitly reject heavy-traffic asymptotics as intractable and use simulation). Re-phrase the gate at the variability level and add Blumstein & Larson.

### G4 — `C3_engineering_16`: quantitative evolutionary models of real software exist, and Muller's ratchet *has* been applied to code.

## 6. Overall assessment: are C3's novelty gates trustworthy?

**Yes for Class B, with four specific patches. No for Class A in five items until the mathematics is fixed.**

**What is solid.**
- **Zero fabrications across ~155 checked claims.** Every named author, paper, book, journal and year that could be checked exists. For a benchmark whose scoring depends on prior-art assertions being real, this is the load-bearing result: no C3 item will wrongly deny an agent novelty credit by citing a phantom paper. The obscure and easily-invented-looking entries — Baianu 1973, Ira Leuthäusser 1987, Lehman's 1974 inaugural lecture, Kellendonk's pattern-equivariant cohomology, Tsallis's 1982 majority-rule RG — all check out.
- **`C3_law_policy_15` is now fully verified** and is the best-constructed item in the set. All 13 prior-art entries check out; the Galam characterisation ("closest prior art, but the object is opinion aggregation, not competence allocation") is precisely accurate; the RG mathematics in its hard rules is correct in every particular, including the subtle non-invertibility/semigroup point; and its central negative claim survives ten independent query forms. The prior audit's flag is cleared. Its one weakness is the unnamed "legal entropy" entry, which cannot function as a gate.
- **Negative claims are unusually well-founded.** Six of seven survived stress-testing across arXiv, OpenAlex, Crossref, DBLP, Semantic Scholar and web search. C3's designers picked genuinely empty niches, not merely unfamiliar ones — this is the hardest part of building a novelty benchmark and they largely got it right.
- Error density is low and clustered: most items have one or two wording-level slips. The 2019 → 2026 citation surface is accurate to a degree that is uncommon in hand-built benchmarks.

**What is not trustworthy yet.**

1. **Five items contain Class A errors severe enough to punish a solver who is *right*.** This is the worst failure mode for a novelty benchmark, because the hard rules are pass/fail gates.
   - `C3_engineering_16` calls μ/s a mutation **load** when it is a **frequency**, and then draws an inference ("cannot be driven to zero by effort on the numerator alone") that is *backwards* under the load reading — where Haldane–Muller gives load = μ, independent of s. The row appears twice, in both `expert_baseline` and `known_answers`. A solver who derives Haldane–Muller correctly is graded against the rubric. It also misattributes the ~2s fixation result to Kimura rather than Haldane, while a hard rule demands three *primary* popgen sources.
   - `C3_economics_business_14`'s own recommended high-scoring answer (writhe as a control-neutrality signal) violates the item's own hard rule R2, because writhe is not Reidemeister-invariant; and R4 mandates a definition of unknotting number (edge removals) that is simply wrong (crossing switches).
   - `C3_materials_chemistry_39`'s "relational core" mixes cellular sheaves with sheaves on a topological space — "overlapping cells", "gluing axiom" — so a solver who uses the correct incidence formulation departs from the prescribed structure.
   - `C3_arts_humanities_41`'s Cheeger statement is one-sided and mixes normalizations, obscuring precisely the direction (small gap ⟹ bottleneck) that the item wants to do humanistic work.
   - `C3_materials_chemistry_13` builds an expected-answer row on Fisher's theorem as a *bound*, without the constant-fitness/additive restriction that makes that reading valid.

   These are not cosmetic: **M0–M4 should be fixed before public release.** M0 is the most serious because it is self-contradictory rather than merely imprecise.

2. **One novelty gate is materially wrong, not merely loose.** `C3_arts_humanities_17`'s claim that gauge-theoretic treatments of meaning exist only as "the invariance HEADLINE only — no connection, no curvature" is false as of the item's own dating. A pre-cutoff paper (Coda et al. 2022) already builds fiber bundles whose fibers are sets of admissible interpretations, motivated by linguistic ambiguity — essentially the item's expected-baseline row — and a 2026 line measures holonomy on semantic spaces as an obstruction. The gate will over-reward. This is the one gate I would call untrustworthy as written.

3. **Three gates are over-broad rather than wrong** (`_39` sheaf-Laplacian-on-molecules; `_40` feedback-in-case-flow; `_16` statistical-physics models of real software plus Muller's-ratchet-in-Avida). All three are repaired by narrowing the claim and adding one or two citations. None invalidates its item.

4. **A systematic pattern worth noting: several `known_answers` entries bundle two or three attributions into one citation that supports only the first.** Examples: "Hansen-Ghrist 2019" credited with sensor networks and opinion dynamics (both separate papers); "Baldwin & Klemperer … and the product-mix auction" (the auction is Klemperer's alone); "Citti-Petitot-Sarti" (the 2006 paper is Citti & Sarti); "Kellendonk 2003; Sadun, Anderson-Putnam" pattern-equivariant cohomology (Anderson–Putnam is substitution-tiling C\*-algebras); "Lehman 1974" paired with 1996 wording; "Perturb-seq / CRISP-seq / CROP-seq (2016)" (CROP-seq is 2017). Each is individually minor, but collectively they mean a solver checking the gate against the actual literature will find the gate's own citations don't say what the gate says — a credibility problem for a benchmark that asks solvers to differentiate themselves from prior art. **A judge scoring novelty against these entries can be argued out of a correct ruling.**

5. **Two entries are unverifiable as written** because they name no work at all: `C3_law_policy_15`'s "recurring legal entropy essays" and `C3_arts_humanities_17`'s "scattered metaphorical remarks". Prose describing an *attractor* is useful design intent, but it cannot function as a citable novelty gate — an agent cannot differentiate itself from an unnamed literature, and a judge cannot verify that it did.

**Bottom line.** C3's prior-art scaffolding is real and largely accurate — the novelty gates are trustworthy in the sense that matters most: nothing is invented, and six of seven negative claims survive live stress-testing. The blocking issues are the five mathematical errors (M0–M4), which turn hard rules against correct answers, and the one over-permissive gate (G1). All are localised and fixable with the replacements in §3; none requires redesigning an item.
