Literature review · October 26, 2026

Overall creativity judgment

Use one overall A/B decision for the final ranking, with New, Useful, and Surprising considered together. This matches the question we want to answer: which paper makes the stronger creative contribution?

The studies below support considering these qualities together. They do not show that this exact LLM judging method is more accurate or reliable than averaging three separate criterion winners on scientific papers.

Two different questions

ApproachWhat the result means
One joint creativity winnerWhich contribution is stronger overall, considering the qualities and their tradeoffs?
Three criterion winners, averaged equallyWhich paper wins more criterion comparisons, with each criterion given the same vote?

Those answers can differ. A paper might have a slight advantage in New and Surprising but a serious weakness in Useful. Equal binary voting still gives it two of three wins. That follows from the voting rule; it is not evidence that the paper is more creative overall. A joint judgment can consider the seriousness of the weakness, although the prompt alone does not guarantee good judgment.

What the studies show

  • Novelty and usefulness can have different influence. Diedrich and colleagues found that novelty mattered more, and that its effect depended on usefulness. Raters were told to consider both qualities; the study did not test LLM judges, scientific papers, or surprise. Diedrich et al. (2015).
  • The task changes what matters. Usefulness contributed more to creativity ratings of real urban-planning proposals than to alternate-use ideas. Participants rated the separate qualities before overall creativity. The findings suggest that the task affects how people combine the qualities; they do not establish that separate ratings should be avoided. Lloyd-Cox et al. (2022).
  • Surprise can add information. Acar and colleagues found that surprise helped explain creativity ratings after accounting for originality and value. This supports including surprise, but does not establish a one-third weight or a scoring rule for scientific papers. Only the primary abstract was available for this review. Acar et al. (2017).

The strongest case for separate judgments

Separate criteria make tradeoffs easier to inspect and can reveal a novel but unsupported proposal. In HD-Eval, rating separate criteria and learning how to combine them from human ratings improved agreement with humans on language-evaluation tasks. That supports a learned combination of criteria; it does not validate equal averaging of three binary creativity choices. Liu et al. (2024).

Joint judging has its own risk: judges can give the qualities different importance. Requiring a reason that discusses N/U/S and relevant tradeoffs lets readers inspect the choice, but does not prove it is correct.

What this means for CREST and AIS

  1. Use the same N/U/S definitions and request one reason and one overall winner. Do not count criterion wins or apply an equal-thirds formula.
  2. Keep both judge models and both A/B orders. Reversing order helps assess position effects; pairwise-judging research does not establish that the joint N/U/S format itself is superior. Zheng et al. (2023).
  3. Average overall votes within each matchup, then weight opponents equally. This handles unequal comparison volume without turning N/U/S into numerical scores.
  4. Report judge disagreement, order sensitivity, and sampling limits. Validate the method against blinded domain-expert preferences before claiming human-level agreement on scientific creativity.

The remaining empirical question: does joint judging predict expert preferences better than separate judgments for these papers and these models? Neither the literature reviewed nor this pipeline run answers that comparison. A targeted human-validation study would be needed.
