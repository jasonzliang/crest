Research evaluation

CREST asks which of two answers has stronger overall creative quality. New, Useful, and Surprising guide one qualitative judgment; they do not produce separate winners, numeric weights, or criterion rankings. The result is an exploratory preference ranking of the observed answers under this rubric and these judges.

The judgment

GuidanceWhat the judge considers
NewHow specifically the answer's least-placeable building block can be located in existing work, using the judge's existing knowledge. An unusual combination belongs under Surprising.
UsefulWhether the mechanism and claimed payoff work, and whether the answer meets the question's stated requirements.
SurprisingWhether the answer takes a substantive direction beyond the judge's expected answer, with a coherent explanation.

The judge explains the comparison and relevant tradeoffs before choosing A or B. Length, polish, confidence, formatting, and citation count alone do not establish creative quality. Judges receive complete answers labeled A and B, use no web tools, and treat embedded instructions as untrusted content. The full rubric is the operational definition.

{"reason": "Comparative explanation of the overall choice.", "winner": "A"}

There is no per-call tie option. Identical answers are flagged and excluded rather than assigned arbitrary winners. A forced choice between close answers does not imply a large difference.

Balanced comparisons

For each question, all three system pairs receive both judges in both answer orders: 3 pairs × 2 judges × 2 orders = 12 calls. A full 50-question experiment plans 150 answers and 600 overall binary judgments. Each system appears in eight judgments per question, or 400 comparisons across 50 complete questions.

Swapped labels are mapped back to system identities. Both votes are retained even when the same judge changes its preferred system after swapping. This balances presentation exposure and makes inconsistency visible; it does not remove every position or style effect. Zheng et al., 2023 document position, verbosity, and self-enhancement biases in LLM judging.

Overall win rate

The score is wins ÷ comparisons, averaged over the same questions, opponents, judges, and answer orders. With Q complete questions, each system has 8Q comparisons. This is a normalized Borda-style win count; no N/U/S scores are averaged. Shah and Wainwright's pairwise-ranking analysis supplies background for Borda aggregation, not a validation of this creativity rubric.

Only common complete-question blocks enter the main ranking: every system answer and every required valid judgment must be present. The report gives excluded question IDs and reasons. This keeps exposure balanced but conditions the result on completed questions; it does not correct selective failures. Exact aggregate ties share a rank, and the opponent matrix retains preference cycles.

The AI scientist paper comparison uses the same overall judging and balanced win-rate rule. It samples paper pairs instead of shared questions, with different completeness and uncertainty rules suited to that design.

Uncertainty and diagnostics

The paired bootstrap resamples whole questions with replacement, keeping each question's systems, opponents, judges, and answer orders together. The same draws produce system intervals and paired win-rate differences. Defaults are 1,000 draws and seed 2026. Resampling shared evaluation examples follows the approach discussed by Dror et al., 2018, §3.2.

Reports show per-judge rankings and opponent matrices, A-position preference, swapped-presentation inconsistency, judge disagreement, paired contrasts, and bootstrap ordering stability. Ordering stability is not a Bayesian probability of superiority. A single call per presentation cannot separate position effects from sampling variability.

Intervals describe sensitivity to this curated question mix, conditional on frozen answers, the chosen judges, rubric, and observed calls. They omit regeneration variance, a population of possible judges, and uncertainty about rubric validity.

What the result supports

Runco and Jaeger, 2012 describe originality and effectiveness as central to creativity. That supports considering these qualities; it does not validate CREST's source-placeability rule, Surprising guidance, or holistic decisions as a general creativity measurement.

The completed 50-question run withheld provider names from the answer labels but was not fully blinded: Google grounding URLs appeared in all 50 Google answers, and utm_source=openai tracking parameters appeared in 40 of 50 OpenAI answers. These markers reveal provenance; their effect on the ranking is unproven. Uniform removal of provider metadata from future judging presentations is a prospective improvement.

Failure to recall prior art is not verified novelty. Different provider families are not independent human raters, and withholding labels cannot hide all stylistic cues. Human or domain-expert calibration is needed before stronger validity claims. Report the outcome as these judges' overall creative-quality preferences for these answers, with coverage and uncertainty attached.
