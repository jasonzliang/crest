\section{The Judge and Its Validation}
\label{sec:judge}

A creativity benchmark is only as credible as its scorer, and the failure mode is specific: the \emph{novelty mirage}---LLM judges systematically over-rate machine-generated novelty and prefer model outputs over human ones, while domain experts disagree \citep{sinhahajari2026mirage}. CREST is designed against this threat, and this section reports what that design achieved \textbf{and where it did not}. Every figure below is recomputed from the released run and is reproducible from \texttt{result/leaderboard.json} via \texttt{analysis/stats.py}.

\paragraph{Panel and aggregation (as run).} Scores are the \emph{median} across a three-vendor panel---\texttt{claude-sonnet-5}, \texttt{gpt-5}, \texttt{gemini-2.5-pro}---at pinned model identifiers. A record scored by fewer than two judges is flagged \texttt{judge\_degraded} and excluded from headline means rather than silently averaged. Judging is blind to agent identity: the prompt carries no entrant name.

Two controls we do \textbf{not} claim, because the artifact cannot support them. There is \textbf{no open-weight fourth judge}; with only three vendors the panel has no redundancy, and an open-weight model served on a Google key would not buy independence. There is \textbf{no answer-order randomisation}: scoring is single-answer rather than pairwise, so there is no order to randomise, and an earlier version of this paper claimed the control vacuously.

\paragraph{Leave-one-family-out was measured and withdrawn.} LOFO is the standard self-preference control \citep{panickssery2024own,ye2024justice}, and earlier drafts named it as the default reported protocol. We ran it, and it failed on its own terms. With exactly three vendors, dropping the entrant's own family makes judge identity \emph{perfectly confounded} with entrant family: each entrant is scored by a different pair, so no judge scores all five entrants and no common-judge comparison exists. The confound dominates because judge severity is large---measured paired on the released run, \texttt{gemini-2.5-pro} is $+2.72$ points more lenient than \texttt{gpt-5} and $+3.38$ than \texttt{claude-sonnet-5} on the primary endpoint, while those two differ by $0.65$. Reconstructing the LOFO subset \emph{widens} the between-agent spread from $2.06$ to $3.06$ points. The anti-self-preference control therefore introduced a larger bias than the one it removed.

The reported protocol is the \textbf{full panel}, with \texttt{same\_family} stamped on every judge row so the LOFO subset stays recoverable post hoc (filter \texttt{same\_family == false}) and self-preference can enter a model as a covariate instead of being excluded by fiat. \textbf{That covariate analysis is not yet run} (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).

\paragraph{Reliability: report both models, or mislead.} One-way agreement is poor. Treating raters as interchangeable, $\mathrm{ICC}(1,k)$ is $0.0 / 0.618 / 0.0$ for New\,/\,Useful\,/\,Surprising and Krippendorff's $\alpha$ is $-0.042 / 0.350 / -0.083$; for interval data these are the \emph{same statistic}, so they are one control reported twice rather than two controls agreeing. Only Useful carries signal, and it misses the conventional $0.80$ bar \citep{hayes2007alpha,koo2016icc}.

That model is the wrong one for this panel, because it absorbs systematic rater severity into the error term, and the severity here is enormous: per-axis spreads of $3.32 / 3.20 / 3.74$ points on a $1$--$10$ scale. Removing the judge main effect, the two-way $\mathrm{ICC}(3,k)$ is $0.736 / 0.807 / 0.759$---Useful clears $0.80$---with the judge accounting for $63.2\% / 29.0\% / 68.2\%$ of total variance. The panel therefore \emph{ranks} answers far more consistently than it scores them in absolute terms. We report both, because either alone misleads: the one-way figures read as ``the instrument is worthless'' and the two-way figures as ``the instrument is fine.'' \textbf{The two-way model was chosen after seeing the data and is exploratory}, recorded as a dated amendment in \texttt{analysis/preregistration.yaml}.

\paragraph{The consequence for ranking claims.} The judge-severity spread ($3.2$--$3.7$ points) \emph{exceeds} the entire between-agent spread ($2.06$ points). \textbf{We therefore make no ranking claim.} Per-agent numbers are released so others can audit them, not so an ordering can be read off them. This is the most important limitation in the paper, and it is a property of the instrument rather than of the entrants.

\paragraph{Human validation: NOT PERFORMED.} No human-expert study has been run. Earlier drafts of this section asserted a consensus panel across all five categories in the manner of Amabile's Consensual Assessment Technique \citep{amabile1982cat}, agreement between judge and expert scores measured against a human--human ceiling \citep{zheng2023mtbench}, and validity-stratified reporting in which humans owned \emph{New} and \emph{Surprising} wherever the judge fell below threshold. None of that exists. It follows that we \textbf{cannot} claim the panel is reliable on some categories and not others: with no human scores there is no judge--human agreement to threshold on, so the earlier ``reliable on C2/C3/C4 but not C1/C5'' claim had no evidential basis and has been withdrawn. Obtaining expert scores on a stratified subset is the highest-priority outstanding work; until it exists, every score here is an LLM-judge score with the reliability characterised above.

\paragraph{Discriminant validity: partial, and one figure is contaminated.} \emph{New} and \emph{Surprising} correlate at $r = 0.883$ at record level ($0.946$ pooling per-judge rows). This \textbf{must not be read as evidence that the axes are inseparable}, because the judge prompt used for the released run \emph{told the judge} that the two axes correlate at $r \approx 0.95$---the measurement is contaminated by the rubric that produced it. The clause has since been removed and the prompt version-stamped, but a clean estimate requires re-scoring, which has not been done. Independently of the correlation, \emph{Surprising} is the uniquely lowest axis in $51$ of $250$ records ($20\%$), so it does real work in a conjunctive endpoint whatever its association with novelty. No factor analysis is reported, and discriminance from output length is \textbf{not implemented}: output length is not a covariate in any released analysis.

\paragraph{Grounding.} A factual-grounding check gates scoring: an empty or sub-threshold answer is recorded \texttt{unscored} rather than zero, and an answer whose dated or statistical claims outnumber its citations beyond a calibrated ratio fails. On the released run it rejected nothing---$250$ of $250$ answers passed and the anti-fabrication clause fired zero times---so we report it as a cheap guard that has \textbf{not} been exercised, not as a demonstrated control. It is a cue-counting proxy: it does not verify that a citation supports the claim beside it, so an entrant that fabricates while emitting plausible URLs defeats it.
