                                             A BSTRACT

         Learned optimizers are usually reported by the margin they win by, not by the
         margin their hand-designed scaffolding already wins by. We study a learned
         evolution strategy whose learned outputs are zero-initialized bounded multiplicative
         residuals on a separable CMA-ES core, so the strategy is exactly the classical
         algorithm at initialization. Its 364 meta-parameters are meta-evolved only on
         2–10-dimensional analytic functions, frozen, and the same optimizer is rebuilt
         for a 704-dimensional Brax Ant policy and scored by best-so-far area under the
         curve over 2,000 generations, normalized so the initial policy is 0 and a same-seed
         CMA-ES reference is 1. The strategy scores 1.191 and beats the reference on all
         eight evaluation seeds. However, switching the learned modulation off—which
         provably reduces the same code to separable CMA-ES plus the fixed sampling
         machinery—still scores 1.107, so the anchor supplies 55.8% of the above-reference
         margin. The learned residual is real (+0.083 paired, 8/8 seeds) but conditional:
         it shrinks with budget and reverses sign on final solution quality. Meta-learning
         succeeded in-distribution; transfer is where the gain was lost.


1    I NTRODUCTION
Black-box optimizers for continuous problems are still overwhelmingly hand-designed, and the
design space is large enough that new heuristics appear faster than they can be compared (Osaba et al.,
2021; Houssein et al., 2022). Meta-black-box optimization proposes to replace that manual search:
parameterize the update rule of an evolutionary algorithm with a small neural network, meta-train it
on a distribution of cheap objective functions, and hope that the resulting rule generalizes (Lange
et al., 2023; Gaier et al., 2020; Zhan et al., 2023). The appeal is that the inner tasks can be cheap
analytic functions while the deployment task is expensive—for instance a neural control policy,
whose parameter space is high-dimensional and awkwardly conditioned enough to frustrate even
methods with access to far more structure, such as Markov chain Monte Carlo samplers for Bayesian
neural networks (Papamarkou et al., 2022).
This creates an attribution problem, and it is structural rather than incidental. Learned update rules
are almost never learned from nothing: they are parameterized deviations from an update that already
works. Outside evolutionary computation, meta-learned optimizers such as Meta-SGD keep gradient
descent as their backbone, learning a per-parameter vector that rescales and redirects its step (Li
et al., 2017); inside it, learned evolutionary operators sit on top of the selection, recombination, and
adaptation machinery of the algorithm they modify (Lange et al., 2023). A learned rule built on a
competent classical algorithm therefore inherits that algorithm’s performance for free. If it then beats
an external baseline, the reported margin conflates two very different quantities: what meta-learning
contributed, and what the scaffolding already contributed. Separating them requires a comparison the
external baseline cannot provide—the same code with the learned part switched off. The question
this paper asks is therefore not does a learned optimizer transfer, but how much of its measured
advantage is the optimizer it was anchored to.
We make that question answerable by construction. We study a learned evolution strategy in which
every learned output is a zero-initialized bounded multiplicative residual, g(v) = 2tanh(3v) ∈ [ 21 , 2],
applied to a component of a separable CMA-ES core. Because the final layer of each network is
initialized to zero, g(·) = 1 exactly at initialization and the strategy is separable CMA-ES wrapped in
the fixed sampling machinery. This makes an exact null control available: forcing every gate to 1 for
the whole run recovers the classical algorithm within the same code path, the same fixed sampling

Our contributions are as follows. (i) We describe a 364-parameter anchored residual evolution strategy
whose learned modules are permutation-equivariant in the population and shared across coordinates,
so a single weight vector serves a 16-member population in 2–10 dimensions during meta-training
and a 256-member population in 704 dimensions at deployment, with no learned feature or gate
reading a generation counter or a budget. (ii) We report the decomposition rather than the headline:
against a pinned same-seed CMA-ES reference at 1.000, the identity-gate anchor reaches 1.107
and the full method 1.191, so the anchor accounts for 55.8% of the entire above-reference margin.
(iii) We show the surviving learned increment is genuine but conditional. It is positive on all eight
seeds, yet its share of the above-reference excess falls from 86.5% at generation 1,000 to 44.2% at
generation 2,000, and it reverses sign on final solution quality, where the no-learning anchor finishes
220.7 return higher. (iv) We localize the failure: meta-training worked, cutting final log regret by
50.8% on its training functions and 28.4% on held-out ones. What did not survive was the transfer of
that gain from at most ten dimensions to a 704-dimensional control task.

2    R ELATED W ORK
Discovering optimizers instead of designing them. Meta-black-box optimization treats the update
rule of an evolutionary algorithm as a differentiable-free parameterized object and searches over it
with an outer optimizer. Attention-based genetic algorithms discovered this way generalize across
problem classes without per-task retuning (Lange et al., 2023); related work learns the search
representation rather than the update (Gaier et al., 2020), and broader surveys situate these methods
within learning-aided evolutionary computation (Zhan et al., 2023) and meta-learning generally
(Vanschoren, 2019). The impulse is not specific to evolutionary computation: gradient-based meta-
learning also learns the update rule itself, for instance by meta-learning a per-parameter step vector on
top of stochastic gradient descent (Li et al., 2017). In both families the learned object is a modification
of a working algorithm, which is precisely what makes attribution hard. Our setting follows the
evolutionary template—outer evolutionary search over inner-strategy parameters, frozen transfer—but
is instrumented so that the classical contribution can be subtracted out.

Adaptation inside classical evolutionary algorithms. The mechanisms our learned gates modulate
are exactly the ones that classical algorithms already adapt by hand: recombination weights, step
size, and covariance. The evolution-strategy family and the derandomized adaptation mechanisms it
accumulated are surveyed by Emmerich et al. (2018). CMA-ES, its best-known member, couples
cumulative step-size adaptation with rank-one and rank-µ covariance updates (Hansen, 2016); its


                                                    2
separable variant restricts the covariance to a diagonal and remains competitive in high dimension,
particularly under low effective dimensionality (Yamaguchi et al., 2022); scaling population-based
search to hundreds or thousands of variables is a distinct problem with its own literature (Omidvar
et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2018). A long line of work hand-designs the online adaptation of operators
and control parameters (Elsayed et al., 2017), of the selection rule itself (Rúnarsson & Yao, 2000), or
of an adaptive grid maintained over the search space (Rostami & Shenfield, 2016). Transferring tuned
CMA-ES parameters between problems has itself been studied (Thomaser et al., 2023), and CMA-ES
is routinely extended with surrogates or randomized parameters for applications (Hamdani et al., 2019;
Sauerland et al., 2023). Our residual gates can be read as a learned, bounded, evidence-conditioned
version of these adaptation rules that degrades gracefully to the unmodified algorithm.

Architectures for population-agnostic conditioning. Because a strategy must accept any popu-
lation size and any dimension, the component that consumes the population is naturally expressed
as a permutation-equivariant set function rather than a fixed-width vector; permutation-invariant
attention architectures formalize this (Lee et al., 2018). Components that consume per-coordinate
or scalar summaries can instead be shared across coordinates, which is the route we take for two
of our three modules. Amortized conditioning of a predictor on a variable-size context set is the
same construction used by neural processes and their descendants (Gordon et al., 2019; Jha et al.,
2022), and by closely related amortized-inference transformers (Müller et al., 2021), and the practical
importance of normalizing such inputs is well documented (Huang et al., 2020).
Where a primary source we rely on conceptually is not cited directly, we name it in prose and, where
a survey or tutorial covers the same material, cite that instead (Emmerich et al., 2018; Hansen, 2016).
This applies to the original separable CMA-ES of Ros and Hansen, cumulative step-size adaptation
of Hansen and Ostermeier, the theory of mirrored sampling, the BBOB/COCO noiseless testbed, and
the Brax physics engine.

3.1   P ROBLEM SETUP AND SUCCESS CRITERION

A candidate strategy is an ask/tell search distribution over RD carrying a vector θ of learned
meta-parameters, with 1 ≤ dim θ ≤ 1024. The evaluator is fixed and has two parts. In the first,
θ is meta-evolved on cheap analytic functions. In the second, θ is frozen, digest-authenticated,
and the same strategy is rebuilt for a continuous-control policy search, which is what produces the
reported score. The control task never updates θ or any outer-search state, so the second part is a pure
out-of-distribution transfer test.
The control task is an episodic reinforcement-learning problem whose objective is the return ac-
cumulated over a policy rollout (Kaelbling et al., 1996), but here the optimizer observes only that
episode-level return, and the problem is solved by direct search over policy parameters rather than
by value or gradient estimation. Concretely it is a Brax v1 ant environment with legacy-spring
dynamics and contact-force observations, optimized with a tanh-linear policy over 87 × 8 + 8 = 704
parameters, initialized at the origin, with 500-step episodes, a population of 256, and 2,000 genera-
tions. For each of eight fixed evaluation seeds we take the mean of the best-so-far return curve over
all generations—the anytime area under the curve—and average over seeds. Writing Aalg , Acma and
Ainit for that quantity under the candidate, under a pinned same-seed CMA-ES reference, and under
the untrained initial policy, the reported score is

Two design commitments follow from the attribution question.

Condition on evidence, not on time. Meta-training runs for 50 inner generations in 2–10 dimensions;
deployment runs for 2,000 generations in 704. A feature carrying a generation index, a budget fraction,
or a horizon would risk letting the outer search fit a schedule tied to the 50-generation meta-training
regime; we therefore exclude such features by design rather than by measurement, as no time-
conditioned configuration was run. Every feature we use is dimensionless—normalized ranks, cosine
alignments, path-length ratios against their neutral expectations, success frequencies—and no learned
feature or gate reads a counter or a budget. The one counter in the algorithm is the evidence-driven
stall counter of Section 3.4, which resets on improvement rather than tracking elapsed time.

3.3   T HE ANCHORED STRATEGY

Search distribution. Candidates are drawn in the standard separable reparameterization xi =
m + σ d ⊙ zi , with zi ∼ N (0, ID ), scalar step size σ, and per-coordinate axis scales d ∈ RD     >0 .
The representation is diagonal, so state and compute are O(D) rather than O(D2 ), which is what
keeps the strategy cheap at D = 704 with a population of 256; the restriction follows the evidence
that a diagonal covariance remains competitive in high dimension, particularly under low effective
dimensionality (Yamaguchi et al., 2022). We treat this as a design assumption rather than a demon-
strated necessity: no full-covariance, limited-memory, or low-rank comparator was run at this scale
in this study, and the large-scale literature contains other tractable high-dimensional search methods
(Omidvar et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2018). The one covariance-representation comparator we do run
moves in the opposite direction—pinning the axis scales to 1—and the diagonal beats it by 0.360
paired (Section 4.4).

Three learned modules (364 parameters). All modules use a hidden width of 8 and a zero-
initialized output layer. (a) A recombination residual (201 parameters) is a permutation-equivariant
set encoder over the population in the Deep-Sets style (Lee et al., 2018): a 6 → 8 ReLU layer
per member, a mean-pooled context broadcast back and concatenated, a 16 → 8 ReLU layer, and
an 8 → 1 output. Its output gates the CMA recombination weights, wi ← wicma g(·), followed by
renormalization. (b) Global gates (98 parameters) map 9 scalar features through 9 → 8 ReLU
and 8 → 2 to two scalars that modulate the step-size update and the mean step. (c) A dimension
gate (65 parameters) maps 6 per-coordinate features through 6 → 8 ReLU and 8 → 1, applied
identically to every coordinate, and modulates the exponent of the diagonal covariance update. Total:
201 + 98 + 65 = 364 meta-parameters. No weight shape depends on D or on the population size N ;
the set module is equivariant in N and the dimension gate is shared across coordinates, which is what
allows one frozen weight vector to serve both regimes.

Features. The six member features are a centered rank, a Gaussian-quantile transform of that
rank, a centered improvement indicator, a normalized radial term tanh(∥zi ∥2 /D − 1), a normalized
base-weight term, and the cosine alignment between the member’s noise and the step-size evolution
path. The nine global features are the CSA signal tanh(∥pσ ∥/χD − 1), a rank-one path ratio, an
elite radial term, the spread of log d, a path alignment, and four success rates (three exponential
moving averages at different rates plus the current-generation improvement fraction), each mapped
to [−1, 1]. The six per-coordinate features are tanh transforms of pσ , of pc /d, of centered log d, of
the rank-µ and rank-one terms minus one, and a broadcast global signal. Every feature is scale-free
by construction, which also keeps the network inputs in a well-conditioned range (Huang et al.,
2020); conditioning a fixed parameter vector on a variable-size evidence set in this way is the same
amortization used by conditional neural processes (Gordon et al., 2019; Jha et al., 2022).

3.4   F IXED DISTRIBUTIONAL MACHINERY

Three mechanisms sit outside the learned part and are shared by every configuration below.
Mirrored sampling. The first ⌈N/2⌉ noise rows are drawn independently; each remaining row
is either the exact antithetic mirror of a drawn row or an independent draw, selected by a gate
clip(1 − N s/m, 0, 1) where s is the slowest success moving average. Every row is marginally
N (0, ID ) under either branch, and the gate reads no clock: when the population is improving often,
mirroring is spent sparingly; when improvements dry up, the draw becomes fully antithetic and the
odd component of the weighted noise sum cancels.

sum, because a mirrored pair contributes (wa − wb ) rather than two independent draws. We therefore

3.6   E XPERIMENTAL PROTOCOL

Meta-training. The outer optimizer is CMA-ES with population 256, run for 1,500 generations.
Each outer generation samples 128 fresh analytic tasks: a function drawn from ten BBOB-style
training functions, a dimension drawn uniformly from 2 to 10, a shifted and rotated optimum, and
additive Gaussian noise with standard deviation drawn from [0, 0.1]. Each candidate runs its inner
strategy on every task with population 16 for 50 generations, starting at standard deviation 1.0. A
candidate’s loss on a task is its best observed clean objective; losses are standardized within each task
across the candidate population and then aggregated by the median over the 128 tasks, so no single
task or scale can dominate. Non-finite or invalid tasks receive a large fixed penalty. The outer mean
after 1,500 generations is the selected weight vector.

Freeze and transfer. The selected weights are serialized, hashed, and re-loaded under a runtime
identity check that binds the source hash, the configuration digest, and the evaluator hash. The
strategy is then rebuilt for the 704-dimensional control problem with the deployment population. No
further optimization of the weights occurs, and no outer state is carried across.

Reporting. Each configuration is evaluated on the same eight fixed seeds (10,000–10,007). We
report the score of Equation 1 together with the standard error over those eight seeds, per-seed win
counts against the pinned reference, and paired per-seed differences against the proposed configu-
ration; a paired difference is the honest statistic here because all configurations share seeds, initial
conditions, and budget. We also report an exact sign test over the eight seeds, but always alongside
the win count, because the two-sided sign-test p-value at n = 8 is identical for a configuration that
wins 8/8 and one that loses 0/8. All configurations completed the full 2,000 generations on the same
8-device hardware allocation within comparable wall-clock times, so no comparison is confounded
by truncated runs. Following common practice in metaheuristic experimentation (Osaba et al., 2021),
we fix budget, seeds, and the reference implementation across all configurations rather than tuning
each separately.


4     R ESULTS

4.1   T HE HEADLINE , AND WHAT IT DECOMPOSES INTO

The proposed strategy scores 1.191 ± 0.019 (mean ± standard error over eight seeds) and exceeds the
pinned same-seed CMA-ES reference on 8/8 seeds (sign test p = 0.0078; paired Cohen’s d = 4.16).
In raw units it improves the mean best-so-far return by 417.5 (95% bootstrap interval [356.7, 486.7],
in return units) and reaches the reference’s final return in 828 generations, a 2.42× budget saving.
Read on its own, this is a successful analytic-to-control transfer of a learned optimizer.
The identity-gate control refuses that reading. Switching the learned modulation off—which, as
verified at the source level, makes every gate exactly 1 and reduces the strategy to separable CMA-ES
plus the fixed machinery—still scores 1.107 ± 0.028 (Figure 2a). Since the reference is 1.000 by
definition, the anchor alone supplies 55.8% of the entire above-reference margin. The paired learning
increment over the same eight seeds is +0.083 ± 0.016, positive on 8/8 seeds (Figure 2b). The
correct summary of the run is therefore a decomposition, not a headline: pinned CMA-ES 1.000 →
identity-gate anchor 1.107 → full method 1.191.
Two further cautions apply to the anchor row itself. Its own advantage over the reference is 7/8 seeds
with sign-test p = 0.070, which is not significant at the conventional level; and because configuration
selection during development used this same eight-seed protocol, 1.191 should be read as the score
of a selected configuration rather than an unbiased estimate of the method.

4.2   T HE LEARNED INCREMENT IS BUDGET- AND AXIS - DEPENDENT

Figure 3 shows why the decomposition, and not the single number, is the defensible summary.
Budget. The two configurations approach the reference from opposite sides. The proposed configu-
ration starts far above it and decays toward it as the budget grows, from an anytime score of 1.663
at generation 250 to 1.191 at 2,000. The identity-gate anchor starts below the reference at 0.897,
crosses it between generations 500 and 1,000, and keeps rising to 1.107 at 2,000, moving further
above the reference over exactly the window in which the proposed configuration is falling toward it.
The share of the above-reference excess attributable to the learned part therefore falls monotonically,
from 86.5% at generation 1,000 to 64.8% at 1,500 and 44.2% at 2,000, while the anchor’s share
rises correspondingly. Extrapolating the trend is not warranted, but within the measured window the
learned contribution is not a fixed property of the strategy—it is a property of the strategy at a given
budget.
Metric axis. The score of Equation 1 integrates the whole best-so-far curve and therefore rewards
fast early progress. On final solution quality the ordering inverts. The proposed configuration finishes
at 3488.5 mean best return, the identity-gate anchor at 3709.2: the anchor ends 220.7 higher, and its
return curve overtakes the proposed one at generation 1216 and never falls back (Figure 3b). Per-seed
final returns were logged only against the pinned reference, which both configurations beat on most
seeds (7/8 for the anchor, 8/8 for the proposed configuration), so this reversal rests on the mean gap
and the crossover rather than on a paired seed count. The learned modulation buys a faster approach
and a worse destination. A study that reported only the anytime score, or only the final return, would
have reported opposite conclusions from the same eight runs.

The diagonal covariance is essential, not inert. Pinning the axis scales to 1 costs 0.360 paired

4.5   A NCHORING AS MOTIVATION , NOT AS A CONTROLLED MEASUREMENT

The unanchored learned strategy scores 0.426 and never reaches the reference on any seed, ending at
2007.5 return against the reference’s 3256.9. The contrast with 1.191 is the intuition that motivated
anchoring in the first place, and Figure 3a shows it is not a slow-start artifact: that configuration’s
anytime score rises monotonically and still ends far below 1. We deliberately decline to read the 0.765
gap as the causal effect of anchoring. That configuration differs from the proposed one in parameter
count (142 versus 364), in module architecture, and in feature design at once; the parameter-matched
unanchored comparison that would identify the effect was not run. What the run does establish
cleanly is the opposite direction of the same question—that the anchor by itself already accounts for
most of what the anchored system achieves.


5     L IMITATIONS AND T HREATS TO VALIDITY

One meta-training run per configuration. Every configuration was meta-evolved once. The
eight seeds are evaluation seeds inside that single run, so every standard error we report excludes
meta-training-seed variance, which is plausibly the larger source. Reported dispersion is therefore a
lower bound on the true uncertainty.
Comparisons confound mechanism with meta-training outcome. Every configuration, including
the identity-gate control, was meta-evolved in its own separate run, and three of them—the isotropic
step-size-only baseline and the no-mirroring and no-path-normalization ablations—landed at weights
measurably different from the proposed configuration’s: the recorded distance from the anchor
point is 20.62 for the proposed configuration and 19.12, 13.50 and 9.90 for those three. For them,
the reported delta mixes “this mechanism was removed” with “a different single meta-training run
happened.” The no-stall-re-expansion ablation is the exception among the configurations whose gates
are active: its recorded distance is 20.62, matching the proposed configuration’s to full precision,
so for that row the confound does not apply and the delta is closest to a clean mechanism removal.
We note this as an observation about the recorded weights and do not use it to argue anything about
the other rows. The identity-gate control is immune to the confound by construction, because its
meta-parameters provably cannot affect any update—which is another reason it, rather than the
ablation table, carries the paper’s claim.
One environment, one policy class, one budget regime. All transfer evidence comes from a single
Brax Ant task with a tanh-linear policy at D = 704. We have shown the conclusion is sensitive to
the budget and to the metric; we have not shown it is stable across environments, policy classes, or
population sizes. The 2,000-generation window also sits close to where the two curves cross, so the
sign of the “learning helps” conclusion is not robust to a modest change in horizon.
Selection and reporting. The eight-seed protocol used to report the headline was also used to select
the configuration during development, so the reported score carries a selection bias of unquantified
size. The sign-test p-value we quote is direction-agnostic at n = 8 and is only interpretable next to
the win count. Finally, the analytic probes and the control task are different measurement families,
and we have kept them on separate axes rather than merging them into a single figure.
Interpretability of the learned part. Bounded multiplicative residuals around an identity make
the learned component unusually legible for a learned optimizer—its worst-case effect is a factor
of two on a named quantity—but we have not extracted a human-readable account of what the
364 parameters encode, and the gate diagnostics available in this study were recorded only for the
configuration in which the gates are inert. Learned components embedded in otherwise transparent
algorithms remain black boxes in the sense studied by the explainability literature (Guidotti et al.,
2018; Arrieta et al., 2020), and the anchored design mitigates the risk rather than removing it.


6     C ONCLUSION

We asked how much of a frozen-transferred learned evolution strategy’s advantage is produced by
meta-learning and how much by the classical algorithm it is anchored to, and we built the strategy
so that the question has an exact answer. Against a pinned same-seed CMA-ES reference at 1.000,
the full method scores 1.191 and the identity-gate anchor—the same code with every learned gate


                                                  10
provably pinned to one—scores 1.107. The anchor supplies 55.8% of the above-reference margin.
The remaining increment is real and consistent across seeds, but it shrinks as the budget grows and
reverses sign on final solution quality.
The negative result is specific and, we think, more useful than the positive one. Meta-training on cheap
analytic functions clearly worked: it halved final log regret in-distribution and improved held-out
analytic functions by nearly thirty percent. What did not survive was the transport of that gain to a
704-dimensional control policy, where it degraded into a budget-dependent head start. Anchoring is
the design choice that bounds how far that failure can propagate—no gate can move a quantity by
more than a factor of two in a generation, and the classical algorithm remains exactly recoverable
as a control. It does not guarantee that the anchored system matches that algorithm, and here it
did not: the full method finished 220.7 return below the identity-gate anchor. The one unanchored
configuration we ran under the same protocol collapsed to 0.426, but it also differs in parameter
count and architecture, so we report it as motivation rather than as a measurement of what anchoring
contributes—an identification this study does not provide.
The methodological point generalizes beyond evolution strategies, and beyond this benchmark.
Whenever a learned component is layered onto a competent classical procedure—and gradient-free
optimizers of this family are deployed that way in settings from engineering design to variational
quantum algorithms (Cerezo et al., 2021; Hamdani et al., 2019)—the honest experiment is the one
that can switch the learned part off exactly, on the same code path, and report the two numbers side
by side. When such a control is cheap to build, as it is for zero-initialized bounded residuals, there is
little excuse for reporting only the headline.
